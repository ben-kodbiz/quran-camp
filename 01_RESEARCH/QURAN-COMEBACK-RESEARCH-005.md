## 1. Primary Qur'anic Passage: Surah Ash-Sharh (94:1–8)

* **Arabic Uthmani:**  
  $$\text{أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ لَكَ صَدْرَكَ (1) وَوَضَعْنَا عَنكَ وِزْرَكَ (2) الَّذِي أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ (3) وَرَفَعْنَا لَكَ ذِكْرَكَ (4) فَإِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا (5) إِنَّ مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا (6) فَإِذَا فَرَغْتَ فَانصَبْ (7) وَإِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ فَارْغَب (8)}$$
* **Standard Translation (The Clear Quran):**  
  *"Did We not expand for you ˹O Prophet˺ your chest, and relieve you of the burden that weighed down your back, and elevate your reputation for you? So, surely with hardship comes ease. Surely with ˹that same˺ hardship comes ˹more˺ ease. So once you have completed ˹your duties˺, continue to strive ˹in worship˺, and to your Lord direct your longing."*
* **Linguistic Architecture:**
  - **نَشْرَحْ (*Nashrah*):** To open up, expand, cut open so that light and breath can fill it. The opposite of *dayyiq* (constriction).
  - **مَعَ الْعُسْرِ يُسْرًا (*Ma'a al-'usr yusrā*):** Notice the preposition **مَعَ** (*ma'a* - "with"), not *ba'da* ("after"). Relief does not simply wait until hardship is over; the seeds of ease are born *within* the hardship itself.
  - **Grammatical Rule of the Definite vs. Indefinite:**  
    *Al-'Usr* is repeated with the definite article *Al-* (referring to one specific hardship), while *Yusr* is indefinite (indicating two separate, abundant eases).

## 2. Classical Sunni Tafsir Analysis

### 2.1 Tafsir Ibn Kathir
* **The Athar of Ibn Abbas:**  
  Ibn Kathir records: The Prophet ﷺ or Ibn Abbas (RA) stated: *"A single hardship will never overcome two eases"* (لَنْ يَغْلِبَ عُسْرٌ يُسْرَيْنِ). Because the hardship is one, but the ease is doubled.
* **Meaning of Relieving the Burden:**  
  Allah relieved the emotional and spiritual weight that was breaking the back of the Prophet ﷺ prior to the arrival of revelation, giving him clarity and peace.

### 2.2 Ma'arif-ul-Qur'an (Mufti Muhammad Shafi)
* **Avoiding Naive Positivity:**  
  Mufti Shafi warns against misinterpreting this verse as a guarantee that life will never have physical difficulties. Rather, it means that for every trial that befalls a believer, Allah creates accompanying internal tranquility, spiritual expiation, and eventual relief that far outweighs the trial itself.
* **The Antidote to Burnout (94:7–8):**  
  When exhausted by worldly striving, true rest is found in worship (*fansab*), turning the soul’s deepest longing (*farghab*) to Allah alone.
