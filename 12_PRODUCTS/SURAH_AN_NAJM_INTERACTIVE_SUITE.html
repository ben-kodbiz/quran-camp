The First Collective Sujood in Makkah (53:62)

In Sahih al-Bukhari (1070, 4862), Abdullah ibn Mas'ud narrated: 'Surah An-Najm was the first surah in which a verse of prostration (Sajdah) was publicly recited in Makkah. The Prophet ﷺ recited it in the courtyard of the Ka'bah until he reached the final verse: *So prostrate to Allah and worship Him!*

Thereupon the Prophet fell into prostration, and every single believer, polytheist, jinn, and human present prostrated alongside him, overwhelmed by the majestic cadence of divine speech—except for an old man (Umayyah ibn Khalaf) who merely raised a handful of dirt to his forehead, saying: 'This is enough for me.' He was later killed at Badr.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 1070, 4862 • Sahih Muslim • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/472) • Al-Wahidi p. 283

The Celestial Ascension & Sidrat al-Muntaha (53:13–18)

The Prophet ﷺ was transported beyond the seven heavens to *Sidrat al-Muntaha* (the Lote-Tree of the Utmost Boundary), so named because all knowledge ascending from earth terminates there, and all divine decrees descending from above stop there before being received.

In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet described the tree being enveloped in golden butterflies and divine radiance of indescribable beauty. His sight swerved not (*Ma zagha al-basar*), demonstrating supreme prophetic composure when gazing upon the greatest signs of his Lord.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 162 • Sahih al-Bukhari 3887 • Tafsir al-Tabari (22/272) • Al-Bidayah wan-Nihayah

No Soul Bears the Burden of Another: Individual Accountability (53:38–39)

Surah An-Najm cites the ancient scriptures of Moses and Ibrahim to establish an immutable ethical axiom: 'No bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of another, and man shall have nothing except that for which he strove (*Wa an laysa lil-insani illa ma sa'a*).'

This permanently dismantles inherited ancestral guilt, tribal blood liability, or vicarious atonement. Every individual soul is judged purely upon its own striving, intentions, and moral choices.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/285) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/465) • Ahkam al-Qur'an (Al-Jassas)

Vast Forgiveness for Minor Lapses: Al-Lamam (53:32)

Ibn Abbas explained *Al-Lamam* as minor inadvertent faults or fleeting inappropriate thoughts that a person does not persist in or glorify, immediately seeking forgiveness.

The verse provides immense psychological relief to conscientious believers who despair over minor shortcomings: Allah does not demand angelic infallibility; He demands avoiding major destructive sins (*Kaba'ir*) and turning back swiftly in repentance, for His mercy is limitless.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4860 • Sahih Muslim 2658 • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (17/110)
