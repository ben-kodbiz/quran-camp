# CHAPTER 4: SURAH AN-NISA (سُورَةُ النِّسَاءِ)
**Classification:** Madani  
**Order in Mushaf:** 4  
**Total Verses:** 176  
**Famous Names:** *Surat an-Nisa al-Kubra* (The Greater Chapter of Women)  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah An-Nisa is the great charter of divine justice and compassion for the vulnerable. While worldly societies routinely exploit the weak, the orphan, and the disadvantaged, Surah An-Nisa places the protection of vulnerable souls at the very center of spiritual life. It reminds humanity that true worship is not proven in isolated rituals, but in how we handle power, family trusts, financial integrity, and justice.

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in Madinah largely in the period following the Battle of Uhud, the Muslim community was grappling with severe social rupture: seventy Muslim men had been martyred, leaving behind dozens of widows, fatherless orphans, and divided estates. In this climate of vulnerability, Surah An-Nisa established legal protections, inheritance shares, marital guidelines, and judicial impartiality to ensure that no weak member of society was deprived of dignity or sustenance.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**Social Justice, the Sanctity of Human Rights, and the Divine Mandate to Protect the Vulnerable.**

---

## 4. Key Passages
1. **The Common Origin of Humanity (4:1):**  
   $$\text{يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ اتَّقُوا رَبَّكُمُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفْسٍ وَاحِدَةٍ وَخَلَقَ مِنْهَا زَوْجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنْهُمَا رِجَالًا كَثِيرًا وَنِسَاءً...}$$  
   *"O humanity! Be mindful of your Lord Who created you from a single soul, and from it created its mate, and through both spread countless men and women..."*
2. **The Command to Render Trusts and Judge with Justice (4:58):**  
   $$\text{إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَن تُؤَدُّوا الْأَمَانَاتِ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا وَإِذَا حَكَمْتُم بَيْنَ النَّاسِ أَن تَحْكُمُوا بِالْعَدْلِ}$$  
   *"Indeed, Allah commands you to return trusts to their rightful owners; and when you judge between people, judge with justice."*
3. **Upholding Justice Against Oneself (4:135):**  
   $$\text{يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُونُوا قَوَّامِينَ بِالْقِسْطِ شُهَدَاءَ لِلَّهِ وَلَوْ عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِكُمْ أَوِ الْوَالِدَيْنِ وَالْأَقْرَبِينَ}$$  
   *"O believers! Stand firm for justice as witnesses for Allah, even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or your close relatives..."*

---

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *4:29:* "O believers! Do not consume one another’s wealth unjustly, but trade by mutual consent."
* *4:41:* "How will it be then, when We bring from each nation a witness and We bring you ˹O Prophet˺ as a witness against these people?"
* *4:148:* "Allah does not like the public utterance of bad speech except by one who has been wronged."

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Universal Family (*Nafs Wahidah*):** Ibn Kathir notes that the surah begins with an all-encompassing address to all humanity (*Ya ayyuhan-nas*), reminding mankind that they originate from a single soul (Adam). From this shared root springs the moral obligation of mutual mercy, family ties (*arham*), and the prohibition of tribal tyranny.
* **Uncompromising Justice (Verse 135):** Ibn Kathir emphasizes the phrasing *Qawwāmīna bil-qist*—not merely being just when convenient, but actively championing equity even when the truth harms your personal wealth, your reputation, or your closest family members. A believer never bends testimony to favor a rich ally or pity a poor opponent; justice belongs to Allah alone.

---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **The Prophetic Tears over Surah An-Nisa:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari* (Hadith 5050), *Sahih Muslim* (Hadith 800): When Ibn Mas'ud recited verse 4:41, the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Stop now,"* and his eyes flowed with tears out of profound awe and accountability.
2. **The Caretaker of the Orphan:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari* (Hadith 5352) from Sahl ibn Sa'd (رضي الله عنه): The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:  
   *"I and the one who takes care of an orphan will be in Paradise like this,"* and he raised his forefinger and middle finger slightly apart.
3. **The Divine Prohibition of Injustice (Hadith Qudsi):**  
   *Sahih Muslim* (Hadith 2588) from Abu Dharr (رضي الله عنه): The Prophet ﷺ reported that Allah said:  
   *"O My servants, I have forbidden oppression to Myself and made it forbidden among you, so do not oppress one another."*

---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Amanah (The Sacred Trust):** Every position of authority, every family responsibility, and every financial contract is an *Amanah* that must be rendered back with complete integrity.
2. **Qawwamun (Guardianship with Responsibility):** Leadership in Islam is never a license for dominance; it is a servant role carrying heavy legal, financial, and moral accountability.
3. **Protection of the Orphan:** Embezzling or consuming the wealth of orphans is classified as one of the most severe spiritual poisons (4:10).

---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **The Tear of Accountability:** When the Prophet ﷺ cried upon hearing verse 4:41, he was contemplating standing as a witness over humanity before Allah. If the most beloved of creation trembled at divine accountability, how can we casually overlook how we treat employees, spouses, or the weak under our care?
* **Justice Against the Ego:** It is easy to demand justice when someone wrongs us. But can we testify honestly when our own mistake caused the harm? Verse 4:135 calls you to stand as a witness *against yourself*. That is the true death of pride.

---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* When entrusted with money, confidential speech, or workplace duties, I treat them as sacred trusts (*amanat*) for which I will answer to Allah.
* When disputes arise in my family or community, I refuse to take sides based on loyalty or prejudice; I stand firmly for what is just.
* I actively look for the vulnerable in my community—the orphan, the widow, the struggling worker—and ensure they are treated with honor.

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Fulfill a Neglected Trust Today:**  
Review your financial and personal affairs today. Is there an unpaid debt, an unreturned borrowed item, or a withheld apology owed to someone under your authority? Fulfill it before the day ends.

---

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. Am I treating those who work under me or depend upon me with gentleness and prompt payment, or do I use my leverage over them?
2. If standing for truth cost me financial profit or social popularity, would I still stand for it?
3. How can I practically assist an orphan or a struggling single-parent family in my neighborhood this month?

## 13. Closing Supplication (Hadith Supplication for Justice and Protection)
$$\text{اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ أَنْ أَضِلَّ أَوْ أُضَلَّ ، أَوْ أَزِلَّ أَوْ أُزَلَّ ، أَوْ أَظْلِمَ أَوْ أُظْلَمَ ، أَوْ أَجْهَلَ أَوْ يُجْهَلَ عَلَيَّ}$$  
*"O Allah, I seek refuge in You lest I should stray or be led astray, slip or be made to slip, oppress or be oppressed, or act ignorantly or have ignorance done against me. Amin."*
