The Epistemic Oath & Banu Salimah's Footprints (36:1–12)

Surah Ya-Sin opens with the disconnected letters *Yā-Sīn*, addressing the Prophet ﷺ with supreme epistemic honor. The oath by *al-Qur'an al-Hakeem* establishes that the revelation is both inherently wise and an authoritative judge over all prior knowledge traditions.

In Ayah 12, Allah states: "Indeed, it is We who bring the dead to life and record what they have put forth and what they left behind (*athārahum*), and all things We have enumerated in a clear register."

In Sahih Muslim (Hadith 665), when the tribe of Banu Salimah desired to relocate closer to the Prophet's Mosque, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade them, declaring: "O Banu Salimah! Stay in your dwellings; your footprints are being recorded! Stay in your dwellings; your footprints are being recorded!" Every step taken toward worship and community cohesion leaves an immutable legacy in the divine ledger.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 665 • Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3226 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir • Tafsir al-Tabari

Habib al-Najjar: The Chivalric Martyr (36:20–27)

When the people of the metropolis threatened to stone the three divine emissaries, a lone man came running from the farthest reaches of the city: "And there came from the farthest end of the city a man running. He said, 'O my people, follow the messengers!'"

Classical commentators (Ibn Kathir, Al-Qurtubi, Al-Baghawi) identify him as Habib al-Najjar (the carpenter). He presented two undeniable rational proofs: the emissaries asked for no wealth or worldly tax, and their moral conduct proved they were rightly guided.

When the mob lynched and trampled him to death, the veil between worlds instantly dissolved. He was greeted with: "Enter Paradise!" Posthumously gazing back at his murderous persecutors, his heart held zero malice, declaring: "Oh, I wish my people knew of how my Lord has forgiven me and made me of the honored ones!" This stands as the Qur'an's supreme portrait of selfless chivalry (*Futuwwah*).

Authorities: Tafsir Ibn Kathir (6/569) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (15/20) • Tafsir al-Tabari (20/504)

Celestial Chronometry: Kullun Fee Falakin Yasbahūn (36:37–40)

Surah Ya-Sin shifts our gaze from earthly martyrdom to cosmic choreography. The stripping of daylight reveals the night; the sun traverses towards its designated destination (*Mustaqarr*); and the moon is calibrated into precise lunar mansions until it returns curved like a dried date-stalk (*Ka-al-'urjūn al-qadeem*).

The cosmic balance is absolute: "It is not allowable for the sun to reach the moon, nor does the night overtake the day, but each, in an orbit, is swimming." (*Kullun fee falakin yasbahūn*).

Imam al-Razi and Ibn Kathir note that *Yasbahūn* evokes fluid motion through space without friction or collision, governed entirely by the immutable decree of the Almighty, the All-Knowing (*Taqdeeru al-'Azeez al-'Aleem*).

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 3199 • Mafatih al-Ghayb (Al-Razi) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (6/577)

Divine Salam vs Criminal Banishment (36:58–59)

The eschatological harvest of Surah Ya-Sin contrasts two definitive states. For the companions of Paradise, reclining upon adorned thrones in perpetual bliss, the supreme crown of their joy is a direct verbal greeting: "Salam [peace]—a word from a Merciful Lord." (*Salamun qawlan mir-Rabbir-Raheem*).

In contrast, the terrifying thunderclap of justice falls upon the persistent transgressors: "Wamtāzu al-yawma ayyuhal-mujrimoon!"—"Stand apart today, you criminals!"

The hypocrites and evildoers who blended into human society on earth are segregated and unmasked. Their mouths are sealed, and their hands and feet bear forensic somatic testimony to every choice they made.

Authorities: Sunan Ibn Majah 184 • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (15/45) • Tafsir al-Tabari (20/532)

The Rotten Bone, Green Fire & Kun Fayakūn (36:77–83)

Surah Ya-Sin closes with the climactic theological dispute over bodily resurrection. When the polytheist chief Ubayy ibn Khalaf crumbled a decaying human bone in his fingers and blew the dust into the Prophet's face, asking mockingly: "Who can resurrect these bones after they have decayed?"

Allah responded with three crushing logical proofs: 1. "Say: He will produce them Who produced them the first time, and He is of all creation Knowing." 2. "He Who made for you from the green tree, fire, and then from it you ignite." Combining opposing elements of water and fire in green wood proves He easily reconciles dust into living flesh. 3. "His command is only when He intends a thing that He says to it, 'BE,' and it is!" (*Innama amruhu idhā arāda shay'an an yaqūla lahu KUN fa-yakūn*).

The surah concludes with sovereign doxology: "So exalted is He in whose hand is the realm of all things, and to Him you will be returned."

Authorities: Asbab al-Nuzul (Al-Wahidi p. 254) • Al-Mustadrak (Al-Hakim 2/429) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (6/592)
