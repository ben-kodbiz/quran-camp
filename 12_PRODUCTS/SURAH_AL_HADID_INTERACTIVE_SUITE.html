The Four Primal Sovereign Attributes (57:1–6)

Ayah 3 establishes the supreme theological foundation of divine ontology: 'He is the First (without beginning) and the Last (without end), the Ascendant (above all things) and the Intimate (knowing all hidden realities).'

In Sahih Muslim (2713), the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ supplicated: 'O Allah, You are the First, there is nothing before You; You are the Last, there is nothing after You; You are the Ascendant, there is nothing above You; and You are the Hidden, there is nothing beneath You.' Classical scholars like Imam al-Bayhaqi highlight that this verse encapsulates transcendent monotheism, refuting pantheism and materialism.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 2713 • Sunan Abi Dawud 5051 • Al-Asma' was-Sifat (Bayhaqi)

The Awakening of Hardened Conscience (57:16)

In Ayah 16, Allah delivers one of the most poignant reproaches in the Quran: 'Has the time not come for those who have believed that their hearts should become humbly submissive at the remembrance of Allah and what has come down of the truth?'

In Sahih Muslim (3027), Abdullah ibn Mas'ud testified: 'Between our acceptance of Islam and the revelation of this verse, with which Allah admonished us, there were only four years!' Classical scholars note that this verse was the catalyst for the repentance of great ascetics like Fudayl ibn Iyad, demonstrating that faith requires perpetual renewal before complacency hardens the heart.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 3027 • Sunan an-Nasa'i • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/35) • Hilyat al-Awliya

The Descent of Iron & The Balance of Justice (57:25)

Ayah 25 pairs ethical legislation with material strength: 'We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain their affairs in justice. And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people.'

Classical commentators from Ibn Kathir to contemporary linguistic scholars observe two realities: the term 'Anzalna' (We sent down) reflects the reality that iron cannot be synthesized in solar cores and was seeded onto earth via cosmic meteorites, and morally, that moral truth (the Book and the Balance) requires protective strength (Iron) to prevent tyranny from crushing the vulnerable.

Authorities: Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/43) • Tafsir at-Tabari (23/201) • Al-Mufradat (Raghib)

The Severing Wall and the Hypocrites' Darkness (57:12–15)

Ayah 12-15 paints a dramatic scene upon the Sirat (the Bridge over Hell). While sincere believers radiate bright light streaming from their hands and chests, hypocrites who feigned faith are suddenly plunged into impenetrable darkness.

When the hypocrites plead to borrow light, they are told to seek light in the worldly life they squandered. A massive wall (Soor) with a gate is erected between them: 'Its interior contains mercy, but its exterior faces torment.' Imam at-Tabari explains that this wall represents the irrevocable separation of sincere conviction from duplicity.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari • Sahih Muslim • Tafsir at-Tabari (23/185) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/33)
