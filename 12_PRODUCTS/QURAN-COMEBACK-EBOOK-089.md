# CHAPTER 89: SURAH AL-FAJR (سُورَةُ الفجر)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 89  
**Total Verses:** 30  
**Famous Names:** Surat al-Fajr, Wal-Fajr  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah Al-Fajr is the epic ballad of cosmic dawn, historical retribution, and the tranquil soul. It opens with five solemn divine oaths: by the Dawn (*Wal-Fajr*), by the Ten Sacred Nights (*Wa layalin 'ashr*—the first ten days of Dhul-Hijjah), by the Even and the Odd, and by the departing night. It recounts the downfall of history's proudest architectural civilizations: Iram of the towering pillars (*Dhat al-'Imad*), Thamud who carved cities into mountain stone, and Pharaoh the master of impaling stakes. It exposes human financial delusion: equating wealth with divine favor and poverty with divine disgrace (89:15–16). It culminates in the most tender, beautiful invitation in all of scripture addressed to the righteous believer: 'O reassured soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing [to Him], and enter among My righteous servants, and enter My Paradise' (89:27–30).

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in early Makkah to reassure the persecuted Muslims that just as night inevitably yields to dawn, the dark tyranny of Quraysh would dissolve before the light of Islam.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**The Sacred Oaths of Dawn and the Ten Nights, the Collapse of Arrogant Superpowers, Re-calibrating Wealth and Trial, and the Return of the Tranquil Soul (*An-Nafs al-Mutma'innah*).**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### The Sacred Oaths and the Vanished Empires (89:1–8)
$$\text{وَالْفَجْرِ ۝ وَلَيَالٍ عَشْرٍ ۝ وَالشَّفْعِ وَالْوَتْرِ ۝ وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا يَسْرِ... أَلَمْ تَرَ كَيْفَ فَعَلَ رَبُّكَ بِعَادٍ ۝ إِرَمَ ذَاتِ الْعِمَادِ ۝ الَّتِي لَمْ يُخْلَقْ مِثْلُهَا فِي الْبِلَادِ}$$
*"By the dawn, And [by] ten nights, And [by] the even and the odd, And [by] the night when it passes... Have you not considered how your Lord dealt with 'Ad—[With] Iram—who had lofty pillars, The likes of which had never been created in the land?"*

### The Fallacy of Wealth as Divine Favor (89:15–16)
$$\text{فَأَمَّا الْإِنسَانُ إِذَا مَا ابْتَلَاهُ رَبُّهُ فَأَكْرَمَهُ وَنَعَّمَهُ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّي أَكْرَمَنِ ۝ وَأَمَّا إِذَا مَا ابْتَلَاهُ فَقَدَرَ عَلَيْهِ رِزْقَهُ فَيَقُولُ رَبِّي أَهَانَنِ}$$
*"And as for man, when his Lord tests him and [thus] is generous to him and favors him, he says, 'My Lord has honored me.' But when He tests him and restricts his provision, he says, 'My Lord has humiliated me.'"*

### The Invitation to the Tranquil Soul (89:27–30)
$$\text{يَا أَيَّتُهَا النَّفْسُ الْمُطْمَئِنَّةُ ۝ ارْجِعِي إِلَىٰ رَبِّكِ رَاضِيَةً مَّرْضِيَّةً ۝ فَادْخُلِي فِي عِبَادِي ۝ وَادْخُلِي جَنَّتِي}$$
*"O reassured soul, Return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing [to Him], And enter among My [righteous] servants, And enter My Paradise."*

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *89:14:* 'Indeed, your Lord is in observation [ever-watchful].'
* *89:21–22:* 'No! When the earth has been leveled—pounded and crushed—And your Lord has come and the angels, rank upon rank...'
* *89:24:* 'He will say, "Oh, I wish I had sent ahead [some good] for my life!"'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Ten Sacred Nights (Verse 2):** Ibn Abbas (RA), Ibn Kathir, and the overwhelming consensus of scholars confirm that *Layalin 'Ashr* refers to the first ten days of Dhul-Hijjah—the most sacred, virtuous days of the entire Islamic calendar, culminating in the Day of 'Arafah and Eid al-Adha.
* **Wealth is a Test, Not a Badge of Honor (Verses 15–16):** Imam Ibn Kathir notes that human psychology mistakenly assumes that financial abundance is proof that God loves them, and financial constriction is proof that God hates them. Allah refutes this: Both wealth and poverty are *tests* (*Ibtala*). Wealth tests gratitude and generosity; poverty tests patience and trust.
* **Who is 'An-Nafs al-Mutma'innah'? (Verse 27):** Ibn Abbas stated: 'It is the soul that was peaceful and tranquil upon the promise of Allah, certain of the meeting with Him, and rested in His decree.' At the moment of death, the angels deliver this gentle invitation, welcoming the believer home.
---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **The Virtues of the Ten Days of Dhul-Hijjah:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 969) from Ibn Abbas (RA):*  
   "The Prophet ﷺ said regarding verse 89:2: 'There are no days in which righteous deeds are more beloved to Allah than these ten days.' The Companions asked: 'Not even Jihad in the cause of Allah?' He said: 'Not even Jihad, except for a man who went out with his life and his wealth and returned with neither!'"
2. **The Recitation over Hamzah:**  
   *Tafsir Ibn Kathir (Hasan):*  
   "When Hamzah or Khubayb ibn Adi (RA) was martyred, the verse was recited: *Ya ayyatuhan-nafsul-mutma'innah, irji'i ila Rabbiki radiyatan mardiyyah*."
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Layalin 'Ashr (The Ten Sacred Nights):** The premier ten days of Dhul-Hijjah representing the peak spiritual season for good deeds.
2. **Iram Dhat al-'Imad (The City of Lofty Pillars):** The lost, technologically advanced civilization destroyed in an instant by divine decree.
3. **Ibtila' bil-Ghirbal (Testing Through Wealth and Scarcity):** The reality that both prosperity and poverty are examination tools, not indicators of divine favor.
4. **An-Nafs al-Mutma'innah (The Tranquil Soul):** The highest spiritual state of the human soul: anchored in peace, certitude, and satisfaction with God.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **My Real Life is Over There (89:24):** Listen to the cry of the person on Judgment Day: *Yaqulu ya laytani qaddamtu li-HAYATI!* ('Oh, I wish I had sent ahead good deeds for my LIFE!'). Notice that he calls the Hereafter *Hayati* ('my life'). He realized that his seventy years on earth were not real life; they were just a brief airport layover. His eternal life is Akhirah. Are you investing in the layover, or in the destination?
* **The Heavenly Invitation (89:27–30):** Imagine closing your eyes for the last time on this earth, and hearing the King of the universe say to your soul: *Ya ayyatuhan-nafsul-mutma'innah! Irji'i ila Rabbik!* Return to your Lord! You were pleased with Him in worldly trials, and He is pleased with you forever. Enter among My chosen servants, and enter My Paradise. Live today so your soul qualifies for that royal summons.
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* I maximize worship and charity during the sacred Ten Days of Dhul-Hijjah (89:2).
* I refuse to measure divine favor by bank accounts, viewing both wealth and poverty as rigorous spiritual tests (89:15–16).
* I cultivate inner spiritual peace (*Itmi'nan*) through constant remembrance and surrender to Allah's decree (89:27).
* I send forth good deeds daily for my real eternal life (*Li-hayati*) (89:24).

Honor the sacred oaths of Surah Al-Fajr: Fast a voluntary day or give intentional charity today, preparing your heart for the sacred seasons of worship.

## 13. Closing Supplication
$$\text{اللَّهُمَّ يَا رَبَّ الْفَجْرِ وَاللَّيَالِي الْعَشْرِ ، اجْعَلْ نُفُوسَنَا مُطْمَئِنَّةً تَرْضَىٰ بِقَضَائِكَ وَتَقْنَعُ بِعَطَائِكَ ، وَارْزُقْنَا أَنْ نَرْجِعَ إِلَيْكَ رَاضِينَ مَرْضِيِّينَ ، وَادْخُلْنَا فِي عِبَادِكَ وَجَنَّتِكَ}$$  
*"O Allah, Lord of the Dawn and the Ten Nights: Make our souls tranquil, content with Your decree, and satisfied with Your gifts. Bless us to return to You well-pleased and pleasing to You, and admit us among Your righteous servants and into Your Paradise. Amin."*
