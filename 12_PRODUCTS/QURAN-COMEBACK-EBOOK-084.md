# CHAPTER 84: SURAH AL-INSHIQAQ (سُورَةُ الانشقاق)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 84  
**Total Verses:** 25  
**Famous Names:** Surat al-Inshiqaq, Idha as-Sama'un-Shaqqat  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah Al-Inshiqaq captures the cosmic obedience of the universe when the sky splits open, listening and obeying its Lord as it must (*Wa adhina li-Rabbiha wa huqqat*), and the earth is stretched flat, casting out all the corpses and secrets buried inside it. It delivers the profound existential truth of human life in verse 6: 'O mankind, indeed you are laboring toward your Lord with [painstaking] exertion and you will meet Him' (*Ya ayyuhal-insanu innaka kadihun ila Rabbika kadhan famulaqih*). It portrays the easy reckoning (*Hisaban yasira*) for the one receiving his record in his right hand, and the horror of the one receiving it behind his back, who lived on earth joyful in sin, assuming he would never return.

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in early Makkah to remind the weary, struggling believers that their exhaustion and sweat in this world is an intentional spiritual labor that will culminate in a glorious meeting with their Creator.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**Cosmic Surrender to Divine Will, the Inevitable Labor of Human Existence (*Kadh*), the Easy Reckoning, and Stages of Life (*Tabaqan 'an Tabaq*).**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### The Exertion Toward the Meeting (84:6)
$$\text{يَا أَيُّهَا الْإِنسَانُ إِنَّكَ كَادِحٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ كَدْحًا فَمُلَاقِيهِ}$$
*"O mankind, indeed you are laboring toward your Lord with [painstaking] exertion and you will meet Him."*

### The Easy Reckoning (84:7–9)
$$\text{فَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ بِيَمِينِهِ ۝ فَسَوْفَ يُحَاسَبُ حِسَابًا يَسِيرًا ۝ وَيَنقَلِبُ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِ مَسْرُورًا}$$
*"Then as for he who is given his record in his right hand, He will be judged with an easy reckoning, And return to his people joyfully."*

### Stage After Stage (84:16–19)
$$\text{فَلَا أُقْسِمُ بِالشَّفَقِ ۝ وَاللَّيْلِ وَمَا وَسَقَ ۝ وَالْقَمَرِ إِذَا اتَّسَقَ ۝ لَتَرْكَبُنَّ طَبَقًا عَن طَبَقٍ}$$
*"So I swear by the twilight glow, And by the night and what it envelops, And by the moon when it becomes full, [That] you will surely embark upon state after state [stage after stage]."*

---

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *84:1–2:* 'When the sky has split open, And has listened to its Lord and was obligated [to do so]...'
* *84:10–11:* 'But as for he who is given his record behind his back, He will invoke destruction, And enter to burn in a Blaze.'
* *84:14:* 'Indeed, he had thought that he would never return [to Allah].'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **What is 'Hisaban Yasira'? (Verse 8):** When Aisha (RA) heard verse 8, she asked the Prophet ﷺ: 'Does Allah not say he will have an easy reckoning? Yet you said whoever's account is interrogated is destroyed?' The Prophet ﷺ clarified: 'That is merely the Presentation (*Al-'Ard*—where deeds are displayed without audit); but whoever has his account scrutinized and audited in detail will be ruined!'
* **Embarking Upon Stage After Stage (Verse 19):** Ibn Abbas (RA) and Mujahid explain *Latarkabunna tabaqan 'an tabaq*: Human life transitions through continuous, rigorous evolutionary stages: from a sperm drop to a clot, infancy to childhood, youth to gray-haired old age, life to the grave, and the grave to resurrection.
---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **The Explanation of the Easy Reckoning:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 103) from Aisha (RA):*  
   "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'Whoever is interrogated in the reckoning will be punished.' Aisha said: 'Did not Allah say: *He will be judged with an easy reckoning?*' The Prophet ﷺ replied: 'That is the presentation of deeds; but whoever is questioned thoroughly in his reckoning will be destroyed.'"
2. **The Prostration in Surah Al-Inshiqaq:**  
   *Sahih Muslim (Hadith 578) from Abu Hurayrah (RA):*  
   "Abu Hurayrah said: 'We prostrated with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in *Idha as-sama'un-shaqqat* (84:21) and *Iqra' bismi Rabbika* (96:19).'"
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Kadh ila Allah (The Sacred Struggle):** The biological and spiritual reality that human life is inherently demanding, designed to be consecrated for God.
2. **Al-Hisab al-Yasir (The Gentle Presentation):** The mercy of Allah pardoning the believer's sins by reviewing them privately without interrogation.
3. **Tabaqan 'an Tabaq (Successive Stages):** The developmental progression of human existence through physical, spiritual, and eschatological phases.
4. **Sajdat al-Inshiqaq (The Prostration of Surrender):** The Sunnah prostration of recitation upon reciting verse 21.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **Life is Exhausting for a Reason (84:6):** Look at the word *Kadih*: It means strenuous, exhausting labor that leaves you covered in sweat. Whether you are a believer or a disbeliever, life on earth is hard work: bills, parenting, sickness, and deadlines. But here is the difference: The secular materialist labors for illusions that turn to dust, while the believer's labor is: *Kadihun ILA RABBIKA famulaqih* ('Laboring TOWARD your Lord, and you will meet Him!'). Let your exhaustion have a divine destination.
* **Returning to Your People Joyfully (84:9):** Picture the believer after his gentle reckoning: *Wa yanqalibu ila ahlihi masrura* ('And he returns to his people in Paradise, radiant with joy!'). All the late nights of Tahajjud, the sweat of fasting in summer, the tears in repentance—in one second, all fatigue evaporates forever. Strive today so you can celebrate tomorrow.
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* I endure the daily exhausting struggles (*Kadh*) of life with patience, remembering that my labor leads directly to meeting Allah (84:6).
* I pray constantly for the 'Easy Reckoning' (*Hisaban Yasira*), asking Allah to overlook my shortcomings without interrogation (84:8).
* I perform Sajdat at-Tilawah with awe whenever I recite or hear verse 84:21.

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Supplicate for the 'Easy Reckoning' in Every Prayer:**  
Adopt the beloved prophetic prayer taught to Aisha: *Allahumma hasibni hisaban yasira* ('O Allah, judge me with an easy reckoning!'). Say it in your Sujud or before the final Salam of every prayer today.

---

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. Is my daily exhaustion (*Kadh*) directed toward worldly vanity or toward meeting my Lord (84:6)?
2. Am I preparing for the presentation of my deeds so I qualify for the 'Easy Reckoning' (84:8)?
3. How do the changing stages of my life (*Tabaqan 'an tabaq*) remind me of the brevity of my time (84:19)?

## 13. Closing Supplication
$$\text{اللَّهُمَّ حَاسِبْنَا حِسَابًا يَسِيرًا ، اللَّهُمَّ آتِنَا كِتَابَنَا بِيَمِينِنَا وَاقْلِبْنَا إِلَىٰ أَهْلِنَا مَسْرُورِينَ ، وَاجْعَلْ كَدْحَنَا فِي سَبِيلِكَ وَطَاعَتِكَ يَا رَبَّ الْعَالَمِينَ}$$  
*"O Allah, judge us with an easy reckoning! O Allah, grant us our record in our right hand and return us to our people joyfully. Make our labor and exhaustion in Your cause and obedience, O Lord of the worlds. Amin."*
