Prophetic Recitation of Surah Qaf in Friday Sermons (Khutbah)

In Sahih Muslim (873), Umm Hisham bint Harithah (RA) narrated: 'I learned Surah Qaf: By the Glorious Qur'an directly from the mouth of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, for he used to recite it every single Friday on the minbar when delivering the Khutbah to the people.'

The Prophet ﷺ chose Surah Qaf because it encapsulates cosmic origination, the intimate knowledge of God, the psychological agony of death (Sakrat al-Mawt), the vastness of Hell (Hal Min Mazeed), and the radiant peace of Jannah.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 873 • Sunan Abi Dawud 1100 • Jami' at-Tirmidhi • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/393)

Closer Than the Jugular Vein: Habl al-Wareed (50:16)

The jugular vein (*Habl al-Wareed*) is the central vascular artery maintaining biological human consciousness. Cutting it extinguishes earthly life within seconds.

Yet Allah states that He is intimately closer to the human being than this vital biological conduit. Classical Sunni scholars (such as Ibn Kathir and Al-Qurtubi) explain that this indicates total, unmediated knowledge of human interiority: He knows the subtle whispers of the soul before the person himself can formulate them into spoken words.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/206) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/398) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (17/10)

The Inquest of Hellfire: Hal Min Mazeed? (50:30)

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4848) and Sahih Muslim (2848), the Prophet ﷺ described this monumental eschatological dialogue. Jahannam, reflecting the severity of divine wrath against obstinate oppressors, cries out for more transgressors.

The Prophet explained that the Fire continues to ask until the Lord of Majesty places His foot upon it, whereupon it contracts and cries: 'Qat, Qat! By Your honor, it is enough!' Sunni theologians maintain this attribute with Tanzih (free from anthropomorphism), affirming God's absolute justice.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4848 • Sahih Muslim 2848 • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (17/21) • Sharh al-'Aqeedah al-Tahawiyyah

The Stupor of Death & The Iron Sight: Hadeed (50:19, 22)

In Ayah 19, *Sakrat al-Mawt* (the intoxication and agony of death) arrives with objective truth. In his final hours on earth, the Prophet ﷺ wiped cold water over his face and declared: 'La ilaha illallah, indeed death has its stupors!' (Sahih al-Bukhari).

In Ayah 22, Allah informs the resurrected sinner: 'You lived in heedlessness of this; now We have removed your veil, and your sight today is sharp as iron (Hadeed)!' Death transforms the invisible truths of scripture into immediate, undeniable optical reality.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 6510 • Tafsir al-Tabari (22/212) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/402)
