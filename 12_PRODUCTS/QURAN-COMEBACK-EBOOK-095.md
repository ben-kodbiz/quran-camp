# CHAPTER 95: SURAH AT-TIN (سُورَةُ التين)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 95  
**Total Verses:** 8  
**Famous Names:** Surat at-Tin, Wat-Tini waz-Zaytun  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah At-Tin is the philosophical and spiritual blueprint of human nobility versus moral collapse. It opens with four sacred geographical oaths representing the great prophetic legacies: By the Fig and the Olive (Jerusalem and the Levant—the cradle of Isa/Jesus), by Mount Sinai (*Tur Sinin*—where Allah spoke to Musa/Moses), and by this secure sanctuary (*Hadhal-Balad al-Amin*—Makkah, the home of Ibrahim and Muhammad ﷺ). It delivers the grand ontological declaration in verse 4: 'We have certainly created man in the best of molds' (*Laqad khalaqnal-insana fi ahsani taqwim*), followed by the sobering fall: 'Then We return him to the lowest of the low, Except for those who believe and do righteous deeds' (95:5–6). It concludes with the ultimate judicial rhetorical question: 'Is not Allah the most just of judges?' (*Alaysallahu bi-ahkamil-hakimin*).

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in early Makkah to refute materialistic cynics who viewed human beings as mere animals governed by instinct. Surah At-Tin affirmed that humanity was created for sublime spiritual perfection, and that denying faith reduces humans to a state lower than beasts.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**The Four Sacred Prophetic Geographies, the Supreme Perfection of Human Creation (*Ahsan Taqwim*), the Fall to the Lowest Depths, and Divine Justice.**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### The Four Oaths of Prophetic Sanctuary (95:1–3)
$$\text{وَالتِّينِ وَالزَّيْتُونِ ۝ وَطُورِ سِينِينَ ۝ وَهَٰذَا الْبَلَدِ الْأَمِينِ}$$
*"By the fig and the olive, And [by] Mount Sinai, And [by] this secure city [Makkah]."*

### The Best Mold and the Lowest Depths (95:4–6)
$$\text{لَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنسَانَ فِي أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍ ۝ ثُمَّ رَدَدْنَاهُ أَسْفَلَ سَافِلِينَ ۝ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ فَلَهُمْ أَجْرٌ غَيْرُ مَمْنُونٍ}$$
*"We have certainly created man in the best of stature [mold]; Then We return him to the lowest of the low, Except for those who believe and do righteous deeds, for they will have a reward uninterrupted."*

### The Most Just of Judges (95:7–8)
$$\text{فَمَا يُكَذِّبُكَ بَعْدُ بِالدِّينِ ۝ أَلَيْسَ اللَّهُ بِأَحْكَمِ الْحَاكِمِينَ}$$
*"So what yet causes you to deny the Recompense? Is not Allah the most just of judges?"*

---

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *95:4:* 'We have certainly created man in the best stature.'
* *95:6:* '...for they will have a reward uninterrupted.'
* *95:8:* 'Is not Allah the most just of judges?'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Four Sacred Locations (Verses 1–3):** Ibn Abbas and Imam Ibn Kathir explain that the four oaths represent the epic centers of monotheistic revelation: The Fig and Olive represent Damascus and Jerusalem (the land of Isa and previous prophets); Mount Sinai is where Musa received the Torah; and the Secure City (*Al-Balad al-Amin*) is Makkah, where Muhammad ﷺ was commissioned with the final revelation.
* **What is 'Ahsanu Taqwim' vs. 'Asfala Safilin'? (Verses 4–5):** Mujahid and classical scholars explain: *Ahsanu Taqwim* refers to upright bipedal posture, symmetrical beauty, intellect, conscience, and the spiritual capacity to mirror divine attributes. But when man rejects faith and follows animalistic lusts, he becomes *Asfala Safilin* (the lowest of the low)—more destructive, savage, and degraded than any wild animal.
---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **The Prophet's Recitation of Surah At-Tin in Travel:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 767) from Al-Bara' ibn 'Azib (RA):*  
   "Al-Bara' narrated: 'The Prophet ﷺ was on a journey and recited in one of the two rak'ahs of 'Isha prayer *Wat-Tini waz-Zaytun*, and I have never heard anyone with a more beautiful voice or recitation than him.'"
2. **The Response to the Final Verse:**  
   *Jami' at-Tirmidhi (Hadith 3347), Hasan:*  
   "Abu Hurayrah (RA) reported: The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Whoever recites *Wat-Tini waz-Zaytun* until he reaches: *Alaysallahu bi-ahkamil-hakimin*, let him say: *Bala, wa ana 'ala dhalika minash-shahidin* (Yes indeed, and I am of those who bear witness to that!).'"
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Ahsan Taqwim (The Supreme Mold):** The pristine physical, intellectual, and moral architecture in which God created humanity.
2. **Asfala Safilin (The Lowest of the Low):** The moral and spiritual degeneration into Hellfire when humanity rejects faith and worships base instincts.
3. **Al-Balad al-Amin (The Sanctuary of Security):** The sacred inviolable sanctuary of Makkah consecrated as a place of universal peace.
4. **Ahkam al-Hakimin (The Most Just of Judges):** The supreme attribute of Allah ensuring absolute, flawless justice for all souls.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **You Were Created in the Best Form (95:4):** Never hate your body, your appearance, or your biological design: *Laqad khalaqnal-insana fi ahsani taqwim.* The Master Craftsman designed your symmetry, your brain, your hands, and your posture with supreme intentionality. But remember: Physical beauty without faith decays into *Asfala Safilin*. True beauty is living up to your divine potential through faith and righteous deeds.
* **Bala, Wa Ana 'Ala Dhalika Minash-Shahidin (95:8):** When the surah concludes with: *Alaysallahu bi-ahkamil-hakimin?* ('Is not Allah the most just of judges?'), train your tongue to answer: *Bala!* (Yes indeed!). In a world filled with unpunished war crimes, corporate corruption, and broken judicial systems, rest in the certainty that the Supreme Court of the Universe will convene. Every injustice will be rectified; not an atom of good or evil will be forgotten.
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* I honor my physical and spiritual creation (*Ahsan Taqwim*) by guarding my health, modesty, and character (95:4).
* I anchor my life upon faith and righteous deeds, protecting myself from sliding into the lowest depths (*Asfala Safilin*) (95:5–6).
* Whenever I recite verse 95:8, I respond with the Sunnah declaration: *Bala, wa ana 'ala dhalika minash-shahidin*.

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Say 'Bala' to the Final Verse of Surah At-Tin:**  
Recite Surah At-Tin today. When you read the final verse *Alaysallahu bi-ahkamil-hakimin*, immediately whisper the sunnah reply: *Bala, wa ana 'ala dhalika minash-shahidin* (Yes indeed, and I bear witness to that!).

---

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. Am I living up to my divine potential of *Ahsan Taqwim* (the best mold), or living for base animal desires (95:4–5)?
2. How does knowing that Allah is *Ahkam al-Hakimin* (the most just judge) comfort me when facing injustice (95:8)?
3. What righteous deeds am I actively doing to secure an uninterrupted reward (*Ajrun ghayru mamnun*) (95:6)?

---

## 13. Closing Supplication
$$\text{بَلَىٰ وَأَنَا عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ مِنَ الشَّاهِدِينَ ، اللَّهُمَّ يَا أَحْكَمَ الْحَاكِمِينَ ، كَمَا خَلَقْتَنَا فِي أَحْسَنِ تَقْوِيمٍ فَثَبِّتْنَا عَلَىٰ أَحْسَنِ الْأَخْلَاقِ ، وَلَا تَرُدَّنَا إِلَىٰ أَسْفَلِ سَافِلِينَ ، وَاجْعَلْ أَجْرَنَا غَيْرَ مَمْنُونٍ}$$  
*"Yes indeed, and I am among those who bear witness to that! O Allah, O Most Just of judges: Just as You created us in the best stature, make us steadfast upon the best character. Do not return us to the lowest of the low, and grant us an uninterrupted reward. Amin."*
