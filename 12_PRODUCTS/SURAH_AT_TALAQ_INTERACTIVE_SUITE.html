The Sacred Architecture of Talaq: Prescribed Term & Sanctuary (65:1–2)

Ayahs 1 and 2 establish the revolutionary ethical jurisprudence of divorce in Islam. The command is addressed initially to the Prophet ﷺ as the representative head of the Ummah: divorce must be performed 'Li-'Iddatihinna'—meaning during a period of ritual purity (Tuhr) wherein no intimacy occurred, ensuring decisions are not made in irrational passion.

Crucially, the Quran forbids evicting the wife: 'Do not turn them out of their houses, nor shall they leave.' The home remains her fully-funded legal sanctuary during the three-month period, providing space for emotional de-escalation and reconciliation. Transgressing this is termed an act of self-injustice (Zulm an-Nafs).

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 5251 • Sahih Muslim 1471 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/149) • Bidayat al-Mujtahid

The Golden Axiom of Deliverance (65:2–3)

Ayahs 2 and 3 contain one of the most widely recited and memorized spiritual assurances in the Quran: 'And whoever fears Allah—He will make for him a way out, and will provide for him from where he does not expect. And whoever relies upon Allah—then He is sufficient for him. Indeed, Allah will accomplish His purpose. Allah has already set for everything a decreed measure.'

In Musnad Ahmad and Sunan Ibn Majah, Abu Dharr narrated that the Prophet ﷺ recited these verses and said: 'O Abu Dharr, if all of humanity were to hold fast to this single verse, it would suffice them for all their needs!' It guarantees that ethical adherence to divine law during marital breakdown never leads to ruin, but activates miraculous divine providence.

Authorities: Musnad Ahmad 21532 • Sunan Ibn Majah • Tafsir at-Tabari (23/445) • Madarij as-Salikeen

The Taxonomy of Waiting Periods & Ubayy's Inquest (65:4)

When Ayah 228 of Surah Al-Baqarah was revealed prescribing three menstrual cycles (Quroo') for divorced women, Ubayy ibn Ka'b approached the Prophet in Medina, saying: 'O Messenger of Allah, some women of Medina do not menstruate—either because they are young, post-menopausal, or pregnant. What is their waiting period?' Thereupon Ayah 4 of Surah At-Talaq descended.

It codified three distinct categories: (1) Post-menopausal women: exactly three lunar months; (2) Women with amenorrhea or pre-menarche: exactly three lunar months; and (3) Pregnant women: until they give birth, whether delivery occurs one day after divorce or nine months later. Classical scholars like Imam ash-Shafi'i emphasize how this verse perfected the legal taxonomy of family law.

Authorities: Mustadrak al-Hakim (2/492) • Sunan Abi Dawud • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/153) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Wahidi p. 288)

The Infallible Guarantee of Ease After Hardship (65:7)

Ayah 7 sets the humane economic standard for divorced maintenance: 'Let a man of wealth spend from his wealth, and he whose provision is restricted—let him spend from what Allah has given him. Allah does not charge a soul except according to what He has given it.' It balances equity with realistic capacity, protecting both parties from unreasonable exploitation.

The verse concludes with an unshakeable divine oath: 'Allah will bring about, after hardship, ease!' In classical Arabic grammar, 'Usr (hardship) is defined, while Yusr (ease) is expansive and indefinite. The great companion Abdullah ibn Mas'ud stated: 'If hardship were to crawl into a deep hole in the earth, ease would pursue it and enter until it extracts it!'

Authorities: Tafsir at-Tabari (23/460) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/156) • Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi)
