# CHAPTER 90: SURAH AL-BALAD (سُورَةُ البلد)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 90  
**Total Verses:** 20  
**Famous Names:** Surat al-Balad, La Uqsimu bi-Hadhal-Balad  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah Al-Balad is the profound social justice manifesto of the Qur'an. Opening with an oath by the sacred city of Makkah (*Hadhal-Balad*) where the Prophet ﷺ was made free of restriction, it states the fundamental existential law of our earthly existence in verse 4: 'We have certainly created man into hardship and struggle' (*Laqad khalaqnal-insana fi kabad*). It then challenges human spiritual cowardice: 'But he has not broken through the steep uphill road (*Fala-qtahamal-'aqabah*)! And what can make you know what is the steep uphill road?' It defines this heroic uphill climb not as mystical isolation, but as active social altruism: liberating the enslaved (*Fakku raqabah*), feeding the starving on a day of famine, caring for the destitute orphan (*Yatiman dha maqrabah*), and encouraging one another to steadfast patience and mutual compassion (*Tawasi bis-Sabri wa tawasi bil-Marhamah*).

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in early Makkah to confront proud tribal oligarchs who squandered wealth in ostentation while ignoring the starving, enslaved, and broken members of society.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**The Reality of Human Struggle (*Al-Kabad*), Scaling the Steep Mountain Pass of Social Justice (*Al-'Aqabah*), and the Companions of Mercy (*Ashab al-Marhamah*).**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### Created into Inevitable Struggle (90:1–4)
$$\text{لَا أُقْسِمُ بِهَٰذَا الْبَلَدِ ۝ وَأَنتَ حِلٌّ بِهَٰذَا الْبَلَدِ ۝ وَوَالِدٍ وَمَا وَلَدَ ۝ لَقَدْ خَلَقْنَا الْإِنسَانَ فِي كَبَدٍ}$$
*"I swear by this city [Makkah]—And you, [O Muhammad], are free of restriction in this city—And [by] the father and that which was born [of him], We have certainly created man into hardship [and struggle]."*

### The Steep Mountain Pass (90:11–16)
$$\text{فَلَا اقْتَحَمَ الْعَقَبَةَ ۝ وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْعَقَبَةُ ۝ فَكُّ رَقَبَةٍ ۝ أَوْ إِطْعَامٌ فِي يَوْمٍ ذِي مَسْغَبَةٍ ۝ يَتِيمًا ذَا مَقْرَبَةٍ ۝ أَوْ مِسْكِينًا ذَا مَتْرَبَةٍ}$$
*"But he has not broken through the difficult pass. And what can make you know what is the difficult pass? It is the freeing of a slave, Or feeding on a day of severe hunger, An orphan of near relationship, Or a needy person in misery [covered in dust]."*

### The Culture of Patience and Mercy (90:17–18)
$$\text{ثُمَّ كَانَ مِنَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْمَرْحَمَةِ ۝ أُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ الْمَيْمَنَةِ}$$
*"And then being among those who believed and advised one another to patience and advised one another to compassion. Those are the companions of the right."*

* *90:5:* 'Does he think that never will anyone overcome him?'
* *90:8–10:* 'Have We not made for him two eyes? And a tongue and two lips? And have shown him the two ways?'
* *90:20:* 'Over them will be fire closed in.'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Meaning of 'Fi Kabad' (Verse 4):** Ibn Abbas (RA) and Al-Hasan al-Basri explain that *Kabad* signifies continuous struggle, strain, and toil. From the moment the umbilical cord is cut, through childhood, earning a livelihood, navigating illnesses, old age, death, and the trials of the grave—man is in perpetual struggle. There is no absolute ease in this world; true ease exists only in Paradise.
* **Scaling 'Al-'Aqabah' (Verses 11–16):** Imam Ibn Kathir and Al-Qurtubi explain that *Al-'Aqabah* is a steep, treacherous mountain pass. In spiritual terms, scaling this mountain requires conquering the greed of the lower ego: using your hard-earned money to liberate the oppressed, feed starving children during economic inflation (*Dhi masghabah*), and embracing the destitute person covered in dust (*Dha matrabah*).
---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **The Reward of Freeing the Enslaved:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 2517) from Abu Hurayrah (RA):*  
   "Regarding *Fakku Raqabah* (90:13), the Prophet ﷺ said: 'Whosoever frees a Muslim slave, Allah will emancipate each of his limbs from the Fire for each limb of the slave, even his private parts for his private parts.'"
2. **The Merciful Are Shown Mercy:**  
   *Jami' at-Tirmidhi (Hadith 1924), Sahih:*  
   "Regarding *Tawasi bil-Marhamah* (90:17), the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'The Merciful One shows mercy to those who are merciful. Be merciful to those on earth, and the One in the heavens will be merciful to you.'"
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Fi Kabad (The Crucible of Struggle):** The reality that worldly life is inherently exhausting and challenging, designed as an arena of moral testing.
2. **Iqtiham al-'Aqabah (Scaling the Moral Summit):** The courage to conquer financial selfishness through social welfare, charity, and advocacy for the oppressed.
3. **Miskinun Dha Matrabah (The Destitute Covered in Dust):** The deeply impoverished human being pushed to the dirt by homelessness, poverty, and abandonment.
4. **Tawasi bil-Marhamah (The Culture of Compassion):** The mutual community imperative to perpetually counsel and inspire one another toward active mercy.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **Expect Life to Be Hard (90:4):** Stop waiting for a day when all your problems, financial pressures, and emotional strains will vanish: *Laqad khalaqnal-insana fi kabad.* This world was never designed as a resort of relaxation; it was designed as a climbing gym. When you accept that life is *Kabad*, your frustration disappears. You stop complaining about the weight and start building your spiritual muscle.
* **Scale the Mountain: Help the Dust-Covered (90:15–16):** Look at the people Allah asks you to seek out: *Yatiman dha maqrabah* (the orphan in your own extended family) and *Miskinan dha matrabah* (the destitute person sitting in the dust, ignored by society). Religion is not just chanting litanies in a private corner; religion is rolling up your sleeves, walking into the streets, and lifting someone out of the dust.
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* I embrace life's struggles (*Kabad*) with patience, knowing that true rest is found only in Jannah (90:4).
* I scale the difficult pass (*Al-'Aqabah*) by investing my wealth to feed the hungry and support orphans (90:11–16).
* I actively cultivate and spread compassion in my family and community (*Tawasi bil-Marhamah*) (90:17).

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Assist an Orphan or Destitute Person Today:**  
Act on *Iqtiham al-'Aqabah* (90:11–16): Sponsor an orphan, deliver groceries to a struggling family, or pay off a poor person's debt today, scaling the steep pass of divine pleasure.

---

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. Am I running away from life's natural struggles (*Kabad*) or facing them with spiritual resilience (90:4)?
2. Have I 'scaled the steep pass' (*Al-'Aqabah*) through real sacrifices for the poor and vulnerable (90:11–16)?
3. Do I inspire and counsel others toward patience and mercy (*Tawasi bil-Marhamah*) (90:17)?

## 13. Closing Supplication
$$\text{اللَّهُمَّ أَعِنَّا عَلَىٰ كَبَدِ الدُّنْيَا ، وَوَفِّقْنَا لِاقْتِحَامِ الْعَقَبَةِ ، اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَهْلِ التَّوَاصِي بِالصَّبْرِ وَالتَّوَاصِي بِالْمَرْحَمَةِ ، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَصْحَابِ الْمَيْمَنَةِ}$$  
*"O Allah, assist us through the struggles of this world, grant us success in scaling the steep pass, make us among those who counsel one another to patience and compassion, and make us among the companions of the right. Amin."*
