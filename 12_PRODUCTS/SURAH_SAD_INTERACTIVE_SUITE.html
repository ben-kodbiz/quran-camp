The Council at Abu Talib's Deathbed (38:1–5)

In Jami' at-Tirmidhi (Hadith 3232, Sahih) and Musnad Ahmad, Ibn Abbas (RA) relates that when Abu Talib fell terminally ill, a delegation of Quraysh notables—including Abu Jahl, Umayyah ibn Khalaf, and Utbah ibn Rabi'ah—visited him, demanding: 'Judge between us and your nephew, that he cease slandering our ancestral deities.'

The Prophet ﷺ arrived and said: 'O my uncle! I ask of them only one solitary word, by which all the Arabs will submit to them and non-Arabs will pay tribute.' Abu Jahl eagerly said: 'By your father, we would give you ten words!' The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Say: La ilaha illallah, and disavow whatever you worship besides Him.'

The chieftains clapped their hands in dismay and walked away protesting: 'Has he made all the gods into one God? This is truly an astonishing thing!' (*Aj'ala al-alihata ilahan wahida*). Thus Ayat 1–5 were revealed.

Authorities: Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3232 • Musnad Ahmad 1/362 • Al-Mustadrak lil-Hakim 2/432 • Asbab al-Nuzul (Al-Wahidi)

Dawud's Judicial Test & Prostration (38:21–25)

While Dawud was worshiping in his fortified sanctuary (*Mihrab*), two men scaled the private walls. Noticing Dawud's apprehension, they said: 'Fear not; we are two disputants, one of whom has wronged the other, so judge between us with truth and guide us to the straight path.'

One said: 'This is my brother; he has 99 female sheep (*Na'jah*), and I have only one, yet he demands I surrender it to his custody.' Dawud immediately ruled: 'He has wronged you by demanding your ewe; indeed, many business associates wrong one another, except those who believe and do good deeds, and few are they.'

Dawud realized this unexpected scenario was a divine mirror and examination of his own conscience. Without hesitation, he sought forgiveness from his Lord, fell down in prostration (*Kharra raki'an*), and repented. Allah pardoned him and elevated him to near intimacy in Paradise. Orthodox Sunni exegesis (Ibn Kathir, Ibn Hazm, Qurtubi) firmly discards corrupted biblical fables concerning Uriah, affirming prophetic infallibility (*'Ismat al-Anbiya'*).

Authorities: Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/57) • Al-Bahr al-Muheet (Abu Hayyan) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (15/164)

Ayyub's Patience & The Cool Spring (38:41–44)

Prophet Ayyub (Job) endured prolonged physical affliction and loss of children with unshakeable fortitude. When Satan attempted to inject despair into his heart, Ayyub turned to his Lord with pure reliance: 'Indeed, Satan has touched me with distress and torment.'

The divine response was immediate: 'Strike the ground with your foot; here is a cool water for a bath and a refreshing drink!' (*Hadha mughtasalun baridun wa sharab*). When he stepped into the spring, his internal and external ailments were completely healed.

Allah returned to him his lost family and doubled their number: 'A mercy from Us and a reminder for those of understanding.' The Qur'an honors him with the supreme testimonial: 'Indeed, We found him patient; how excellent a servant! He was ever-returning in repentance.'

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 3391 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/72) • Ma'alim at-Tanzil lil-Baghawi

The Dispute of the High Council (38:69–74)

The Prophet ﷺ announced: 'I had no knowledge of the exalted celestial council (*Al-Mala' al-A'la*) when they debated, except that it is revealed to me that I am only a clear warner.'

This debate centers on the creation of Adam from clay. Allah commanded the angels: 'When I have fashioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, fall down to him in prostration.' All angels prostrated except Iblis, who justified his rebellion with elemental pride: 'I am better than him; You created me from fire and created him from clay.'

Allah questioned: 'O Iblis, what prevented you from prostrating to that which I created with My Two Hands (*Khalaqtu bi-Yadayya*)?' Sunni theology affirms Allah's attributes (*Sifat*) as befits His Majesty without anthropomorphism (*Tashbeeh*) or negation (*Ta'teel*). Iblis was banished as an outcast (*Rajeem*) forever.

Authorities: Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3233 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/83) • Sharh al-'Aqeedah al-Tahawiyyah
