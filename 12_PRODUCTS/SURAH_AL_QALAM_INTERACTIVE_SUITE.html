The Summit of Human Nobility: Wa Innaka la-'ala Khuluqin 'Azeem

In Ayah 4, Allah swears an eternal oath vindicating the Prophet ﷺ from all pagan slurs: 'And indeed, you are of a magnificent moral character (Khuluqin 'Azeem).' In Sahih Muslim (746), Sa'd ibn Hisham asked the Mother of the Believers, 'A'ishah: 'O Mother of the Believers, tell me about the character of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.' She replied: 'Do you not read the Qur'an?' He said: 'Yes.' She said: 'Verily, the character of the Prophet of Allah was the Qur'an (Kana khuluquhu al-Qur'an).'

Imam al-Qurtubi explains that this means he embodied its commands, internalized its prohibitions, displayed its noble etiquette, took admonition from its parables, and manifested its transcendent mercy across every domain of human interaction.

He was never vulgar, never used indecent speech, never shouted in the marketplaces, and never repaid evil with evil; rather, he forgave and pardoned (Sahih al-Bukhari 2125).

This sublime character was not an effortless natural disposition but an active, disciplined surrender to revelation, serving as the universal prototype for all righteous humanity.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 746; Sahih al-Bukhari 2125; Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/227); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/187).

The Parable of the Greedy Heirs: Ashab al-Jannah & The Absent Insha Allah

Classical exegetes, including Ibn Abbas and Al-Wahidi in Asbab an-Nuzul, relate that near Sana'a in Yemen, a righteous landowner possessed a fertile orchard. Whenever he harvested, he distributed one-third to the poor, one-third for maintenance, and one-third for his family.

When he died, his sons held a secret council: 'Our father was a foolish old man who wasted profits on beggars. If we harvest at early dawn before the poor awaken, we can keep the entire yield.' They swore an oath: 'La-yasrimunnahā musbiheen' (They would surely harvest at dawn) and failed to make exception ('Wa la yastathnoon'—omitting Insha Allah).

While they slept in greedy contentment, a fiery cyclone from Allah (Ta'if) encircled the groves, reducing the emerald fruit trees to blackened, smoldering ashes (Ka-s-Sareem).

Upon arriving at dawn and seeing the wasteland, their leader admitted: 'Did I not say to you: why did you not glorify Allah?' The story demonstrates that wealth hoarded through the exclusion of vulnerable souls is biologically and spiritually flammable.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (23/535); Al-Wahidi, Asbab an-Nuzul, p. 288; Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/193); Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (18/235).

The Terrifying Trial: Yawma Yukshafu 'an Saq & Prostration Paralysis

Ayah 42 warns of the catastrophic Day: 'The Day the shin will be uncovered [Yukshafu 'an saq] and they will be called to prostrate, but they will not be able to.'

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4919) and Sahih Muslim (182) on the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, the Prophet ﷺ explained: 'Our Lord will uncover His Shin, and every believing man and believing woman will prostrate to Him. But there will remain those who used to prostrate in the world out of showing off and seeking reputation. They will try to prostrate, but their spine will become a single rigid bone [Tabaqan wahidan], and whenever they attempt to bow, they will fall backward upon their necks.'

Ibn Abbas explained the linguistic metaphor of 'Kashf as-Saq' in classical Arabic as the unveiling of intense cosmic hardship, when battle becomes fierce and all pretenses are stripped away.

Those who refused genuine, humble prostration when healthy and sound in this world are forensically paralyzed when the divine countenance is manifested in judgment.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4919; Sahih Muslim 182; Tafsir al-Tabari (23/551); Fath al-Bari (8/663).

The Piercing Evil Eye: Layuzliqoonaka bi-Absarihim & The Universal Dhikr

Ayah 51 documents the desperate psychological warfare of pagan chieftains: 'And indeed, those who disbelieve would almost strike you down with their eyes when they hear the message, and they say: Indeed, he is mad!'

Imam Ibn Kathir records that there were men from Banu Asad notorious for lethal envious stares. An individual among them would fast for three days, then gaze upon a camel and say, 'I have never seen a more magnificent beast!'—and the camel would instantly collapse and die. Pagan leaders hired these men to gaze upon the Prophet ﷺ during recitation to destroy him.

Allah revealed these verses as an impenetrable shield, incapacitating their malicious intent and affirming that revelation is preserved by divine decree beyond the reach of biological or spiritual curses.

The Surah closes by consecrating the Qur'an as 'Dhikrun lil-'Alameen' (A Universal Reminder for all the worlds), proving that truth radiates outward to all creation while hatred consumes only its harborers.

Authorities: Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/202); Tafsir al-Baghawi (8/198); Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/254); Musnad Ahmad.
