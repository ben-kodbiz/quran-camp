The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah as 'Fathun Mubeen' (48:1–3)

In Dhu'l-Qa'dah 6 AH, the Prophet ﷺ and 1,400 companions marched unarmed to Makkah for 'Umrah. Blocked at Hudaybiyyah, negotiations produced a 10-year truce with terms that appeared disadvantageous to some companions.

Yet on the return march, Surah Al-Fath was revealed. In Sahih al-Bukhari (4833) and Sahih Muslim (1786), Umar asked: 'Is this a victory, O Messenger of Allah?' He replied: 'Yes, by Him in Whose Hand is my soul, it is indeed a victory!' Peaceful contact allowed Islam to spread rapidly, doubling the community within two years.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4833 • Sahih Muslim 1786 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/333) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Al-Wahidi)

The Pledge Under the Tree: Bay'at ar-Ridwan (48:10, 18)

When rumor spread that Uthman ibn Affan had been murdered in Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ gathered the companions under an acacia tree, taking a solemn pledge to fight to the death and never flee.

Allah declared: 'Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you are pledging to Allah; the Hand of Allah is over their hands!' (48:10) and revealed: 'Allah was well-pleased with the believers when they pledged allegiance under the tree!' (48:18). In Sahih Muslim, the Prophet stated: 'None of those who pledged allegiance under the tree shall enter the Fire.'

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4840 • Sahih Muslim 1856 • Tafsir al-Tabari (22/160)

The Divine Sakeenah (Tranquility) in Islamic Theology (48:4, 18, 26)

Surah Al-Fath mentions Sakeenah three separate times: descending into believers' hearts to increase faith (Ayah 4), rewarding their steadfast loyalty under the tree (Ayah 18), and protecting their composure against pagan tribal chauvinism (Ayah 26).

Ibn al-Qayyim describes Sakeenah as a palpable divine calmness and serenity that settles upon the heart and senses, banishing panic, anger, and self-doubt, enabling leaders and believers to act with absolute poise under immense pressure.

Authorities: Madarij as-Salikin (Ibn al-Qayyim) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/338) • Al-Baghawi

The Two Prophetic Parables: Torah and Gospel (48:29)

Surah Al-Fath concludes with an extraordinary dual comparative parable: In the Torah, the Prophet's companions are described by their devotional humility—bowing and prostrating with visible marks of awe on their faces (Seemahum).

In the Gospel, they are depicted by their dynamic communal growth—like a tiny shoot sprouting, thickening, and standing robustly on its stalk. The parable celebrates both inner contemplative devotion (Torah) and vigorous social establishment (Gospel).

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari • Tafsir al-Tabari (22/178) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (16/295)
