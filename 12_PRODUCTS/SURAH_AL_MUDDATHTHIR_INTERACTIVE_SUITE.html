The Clarion Call to Public Mission: Ya Ayyuha al-Muddaththir (Bukhari 4)

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4) and Sahih Muslim (161) on the authority of Jabir ibn Abdillah, the Prophet ﷺ described the cessation of revelation (Fatrat al-Wahy): 'While I was walking, I suddenly heard a voice from the sky. I looked up and saw the same angel who had visited me at the cave of Hira, seated upon a throne between heaven and earth. I was terrified of him, so I fell to the ground, then rushed back to my family crying: Cover me! Wrap me up! (Zammilooni! Dath-thirooni!).'

'They wrapped me in blankets, and thereupon Allah revealed: 'O you wrapped in your cloak! Arise and warn! And your Lord magnify! And your garments purify! And defilement shun!' (74:1–5).'

Scholars of prophetic biography note that through Surah Al-'Alaq (Iqra') he became a Prophet (Nabi), and through Surah Al-Muddaththir (Qum fa-andhir) he was commissioned as an active Apostolic Messenger (Rasool) to all humanity.

The commands establish the complete moral curriculum of Islamic leadership: active public warning (Qum fa-andhir), monotheistic focus (Wa Rabbaka fa-kabbir), physical and ethical purity (Wa thiyabaka fa-tahhir), complete separation from sin (War-rujza fahjur), and sincere charity devoid of self-interest (Wa la tamnun tastakthir).

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4, 4922; Sahih Muslim 161; Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/264); Zad al-Ma'ad.

Walid ibn al-Mughirah's Fatal Deliberation: Fakkara wa Qaddar (Bukhari 4922)

Classical authorities, including Al-Wahidi (Asbab an-Nuzul, p. 293), Al-Tabari, and Ibn Kathir, record that Walid ibn al-Mughirah (the wealthiest aristocrat of Quraysh) heard the Prophet ﷺ reciting the Qur'an and was visibly moved. He remarked to his tribe: 'By Allah, I have just heard from Muhammad words that are unlike the speech of men or jinn. It possesses sweetness, it is crowned with beauty; its base is abundant and its top is fruitful; it rises above all and nothing can rise above it!'

Fearing that the leader of Mecca had converted, Abu Jahl rushed to Walid and manipulated his pride: 'Your people are collecting money to pay you, for they think you have humbled yourself before Muhammad to eat from his scraps!' Walid was outraged and declared: 'Quraysh know that I am the wealthiest of them all!'

Walid then sat in solitary deliberation to craft a public PR lie for the approaching pilgrimage season. He considered calling him a poet, a madman, or a soothsayer, but rejected each because the people knew Muhammad displayed none of those traits. Finally, after frowning and scowling in pride, he said: 'This is nothing but ancient magic transmitted from olden times!'

Thereupon Allah revealed Ayahs 11–26: 'Leave Me with the one I created alone... He thought and calculated; may he be destroyed, how he calculated!'—consigning his arrogance to the scorching furnace of Saqar.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (23/705); Al-Wahidi, Asbab an-Nuzul, p. 293; Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/268); Dala'il an-Nubuwwah lil-Bayhaqi.

The Mystery of the Nineteen Wardens: 'Alayha Tis'ata 'Ashar

In Ayah 30, Allah reveals: 'Over it are nineteen [wardens].' When this verse was recited, the Meccan polytheist Abu Jahl sneered arrogantly: 'Can your clans not overpower nineteen guards? I alone will take care of ten, and you handle the remaining nine!'

Allah immediately revealed Ayah 31, clarifying the nature of the nineteen: 'And We have not made the keepers of the Fire except angels, and We have not made their number except as a trial for those who disbelieve... and none knows the soldiers of your Lord except He.'

Imam Ibn Kathir notes that each of these nineteen angelic wardens (Zabaniyah) possesses colossal, supernatural cosmic power: a single one of them could pick up the entire population of the earth with one hand. Worldly strength and numerical armies are utterly meaningless against metaphysical angelic beings created from pure divine light.

The specific number serves as a discriminator between true faith and arrogant skepticism: the humble believe in revelation without demanding arbitrary explanations, while the cynical drown in mocking arithmetic until Saqar seizes them.

Authorities: Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/272); Tafsir al-Qurtubi (19/75); Tafsir at-Tabari (23/715); Fath al-Bari.

The Four Fatal Confessions: Ma Salakakum fee Saqar? (74:42–47)

Ayahs 40–47 portray an extraordinary courtroom dialogue across metaphysical dimensions: the righteous dwellers of Paradise gaze into the abyss and ask the criminals: 'What has plunged you into Saqar? [Ma salakakum fee saqar?].'

The condemned respond with four clear, comprehensive confessions that diagnose the complete collapse of religion and ethics: (1) 'Lam naku minal-musalleen'—We did not establish prayer, cutting our relationship with our Creator; (2) 'Wa lam naku nut'imu al-miskeen'—We refused to feed the poor, displaying cold-hearted social indifference; (3) 'Wa kunna nakhoodu ma'al-kha'ideen'—We waded in cynical, mocking gossip with the frivolous crowds; (4) 'Wa kunna nukadh-dhibu bi-yawmi ad-deen'—We lived in intellectual denial of the Day of Judgment until death (Al-Yaqeen) overtook us.

Classical exegetes emphasize that these four points represent the failure of the vertical axis (relationship with God via prayer), the failure of the horizontal axis (relationship with society via charity), the failure of intellectual discipline (wading in falsehood), and the failure of eschatological accountability (denial of Judgment).

Consequently, verse 48 delivers the final verdict: 'Fa-ma tanfa'uhum shafa'atu ash-shafi'een'—no intercession by any mediator can ever avail those who die upon this quadruple bankruptcy.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (23/725); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/276); Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (19/84); Ihya' 'Ulum ad-Deen.
