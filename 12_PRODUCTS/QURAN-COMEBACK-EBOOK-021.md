# CHAPTER 21: SURAH AL-ANBIYA (سُورَةُ الأنبياء)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 21  
**Total Verses:** 112  
**Famous Names:** Surat al-Anbiya (The Chapter of the Prophets)  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah Al-Anbiya is the sanctuary of prophetic crisis and divine rescue. It begins with a thunderous cosmic wake-up call—'Closer and closer to mankind comes their reckoning, while they heedlessly turn away' (21:1)—and transitions into an extraordinary sequence of prophetic du'as in extreme distress: Ibrahim in the furnace, Ayyub in chronic agony, Yunus in the triple darkness of the whale's belly, and Zakariyya in frail old age without an heir. In every single case, Allah answers with *Fastajabna lahu* ('So We responded to him'), proving that prayer is the ultimate catalyst that bends physical reality for the sincere believer.

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in Makkah during intense persecution and social boycott. The Quraysh mocked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, claiming he was merely a mortal poet or sorcerer. Surah Al-Anbiya answered by parading sixteen great prophets across millennia, proving that all messengers were mortal men who suffered, bled, prayed, and were divinely rescued through unshakeable tawhid.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**The Imminence of Cosmic Accountability, the Unity of Prophetic Tawhid, and Divine Rescue Through Supplication (*Istijabah*).**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### The Wake-Up Call of Accountability (21:1)
$$\text{اقْتَرَبَ لِلنَّاسِ حِسَابُهُمْ وَهُمْ فِي غَفْلَةٍ مَّعْرِضُونَ}$$
*"Closer and closer to mankind comes their reckoning, while they heedlessly turn away."*

### The Cosmic Origin of Water and Life (21:30)
$$\text{أَوَلَمْ يَرَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنَّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ كَانَتَا رَتْقًا فَفَتَقْنَاهُمَا ۖ وَجَعَلْنَا مِنَ الْمَاءِ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ حَيٍّ ۖ أَفَلَا يُؤْمِنُونَ}$$
*"Have those who disbelieved not considered that the heavens and the earth were a joined entity, and then We separated them and made from water every living thing? Then will they not believe?"*

### The Cry of Dhun-Nun in the Depths (21:87–88)
$$\text{وَذَا النُّونِ إِذ ذَّهَبَ مُغَاضِبًا فَظَنَّ أَن لَّن نَّقْدِرَ عَلَيْهِ فَنَادَىٰ فِي الظُّلُمَاتِ أَن لَّا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ ۝ فَاسْتَجَبْنَا لَهُ وَنَجَّيْنَاهُ مِنَ الْغَمِّ ۚ وَكَذَٰلِكَ نُنجِي الْمُؤْمِنِينَ}$$
*"And [mention] the man of the fish, when he went off in anger and thought that We would not decree [hardship] upon him. And he called out within the darknesses, 'There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers.' So We responded to him and saved him from the distress. And thus do We save the believers."*

### The Universal Mercy (21:107)
$$\text{وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ إِلَّا رَحْمَةً لِّلْعَالَمِينَ}$$
*"And We have not sent you, [O Muhammad], except as a mercy to the worlds."*

---

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *21:35:* 'Every soul will taste death. And We test you with evil and with good as trial; and to Us you will be returned.'
* *21:83:* 'And [mention] Job, when he called to his Lord, "Indeed, adversity has touched me, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful."'
* *21:90:* '...Indeed, they used to hasten to good deeds and supplicate Us in hope and fear, and they were to Us humbly submissive.'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Universal Promise to the Believers (Verse 88):** Imam Ibn Kathir highlights that after saving Yunus from the belly of the whale, Allah added: *'And thus do We save the believers.'* This proves that the rescue of Yunus was not an exclusive miracle reserved only for prophets; any believer who calls upon Allah with that same admission of weakness, repentance, and pure monotheism in the dark moments of life will be granted divine deliverance.
* **Testing with Ease vs. Difficulty (Verse 35):** Imam al-Qurtubi explains that being tested with 'good' (wealth, health, fame, ease) is often far more spiritually dangerous than being tested with 'evil' (poverty, sickness, loss), because hardship instinctively turns a person to prayer, whereas continuous ease frequently breeds arrogance, complacency, and neglect.
---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **The Infallible Du'a of Distress:**  
   *Jami' at-Tirmidhi (Hadith 3505), Sahih:*  
   "The Prophet ﷺ said: 'The supplication of Dhun-Nun (Yunus) when he called upon Allah inside the belly of the whale was: *La ilaha illa Anta, Subhanaka, inni kuntu minaz-zalimin*. No Muslim supplicates with it for any matter whatsoever except that Allah responds to him.'"
2. **The Virtues of Surat al-Anbiya:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 4707) from Ibn Mas'ud (RA):*  
   "Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (RA) said regarding Surah Bani Isra'il (Al-Isra), Al-Kahf, Maryam, Ta-Ha, and Al-Anbiya: 'They are among the earliest revelations, and they are among my ancient, prized treasures (*al-ʻitaq al-uwal*).'"
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Istijabah (Divine Response):** The immediate divine intervention and relief granted to prophets and believers who call upon Allah in utter sincerity.
2. **Rahmatan lil-'Alamin (Mercy to All Creation):** The ontological purpose of the Prophet Muhammad's ﷺ mission as cosmic mercy for humans, jinn, animals, and creation.
3. **Fitnat ash-Sharr wal-Khayr (The Dual Test):** The reality that prosperity and adversity are twin examinations of gratitude and steadfast patience.
4. **At-Tadarru' (Humble Pleading):** Approaching Allah with complete vulnerability and confession of one's shortcomings.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **The Anatomy of Yunus's Prayer (21:87):** Examine the words of Yunus: He did not ask for a single worldly favor. He did not say, 'Take me out of this fish.' Instead, he proclaimed three things: Monotheism (*La ilaha illa Anta*), Exaltation of Allah's Perfection (*Subhanaka*), and Confession of Personal Fault (*Inni kuntu minaz-zalimin*). True prayer begins by honoring Allah's majesty and admitting your own vulnerability; once the heart aligns with truth, Allah removes the darkness without you even asking.
* **Hasting to Good in Times of Ease (21:90):** Why did Allah answer Zakariyya and cure his wife's barrenness in extreme old age? The Qur'an gives the secret: *'Indeed, they used to hasten to good deeds and supplicate Us in hope and fear.'* If you want Allah to answer you in your moments of crisis, hasten to obey Him in your moments of calm.
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* Whenever I face suffocating anxiety or hopelessness, I repeat the du'a of Prophet Yunus with full contemplation of my reliance on Allah.
* I recognize that ease and comfort are rigorous spiritual tests demanding humility, generosity, and extra vigilance against heedlessness.
* I never write off any personal or professional deadlock as 'impossible', remembering how Allah opened the womb of Zakariyya's wife and cooled the fire for Ibrahim.

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Recite the Du'a of Yunus 33 Times in Sujud:**  
In your voluntary prayer today, stay in prostration and repeat *La ilaha illa Anta, Subhanaka, inni kuntu minaz-zalimin* 33 times, reflecting on whatever 'darkness' or distress is currently burdening your life, and trust in Allah's promise: *'And thus do We save the believers.'*

---

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. Am I living with the acute awareness that my final reckoning is approaching every second (21:1), or am I drifting in digital heedlessness?
2. When trial strikes, do I complain to people about Allah, or do I complain to Allah about my own shortcomings like Yunus and Ayyub?
3. How can I hasten to good deeds today while I still possess health and vitality?

## 13. Closing Supplication
$$\text{لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ ، اللَّهُمَّ نَجِّنَا مِنَ الْغَمِّ وَفَرِّجْ هُمُومَنَا يَا أَرْحَمَ الرَّاحِمِينَ}$$  
*"There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers. O Allah, deliver us from distress, relieve our anxieties, and grant us Your divine mercy, O Most Merciful of the merciful. Amin."*
