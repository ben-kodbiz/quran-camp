The Grand Ontological Charter: Li-Ya'budoon (51:56)

This verse forms the metaphysical cornerstone of Islamic anthropology: 'And I did not create jinn and mankind except that they should worship Me (*Illa li-ya'budoon*).' Ibn Abbas explained that *Li-ya'budoon* means *Li-ya'rifoon* (that they may know Me), for true worship is rooted in deep cognitive and spiritual recognition of the Creator.

It liberates human beings from nihilism and consumerism, establishing that humanity's primary purpose is knowing, revering, and living in conscious harmony with Allah's divine wisdom.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/236) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/425) • Madarij as-Salikin (Ibn al-Qayyim)

The Cosmic Expansion: Inna la-Moosi'oon (51:47)

Surah Adh-Dhariyat contains one of the most stunning cosmological affirmations in scripture: 'And the heaven We constructed with strength, and indeed, We are [its] expanders (*Wa inna la-moosi'oon*).' Classical lexicographers (such as Ibn Mandhur and Al-Raghib) note that *Awsa'a* signifies expanding expanse and capacity.

Modern astrophysics confirms that the physical universe is not static, but continuously expanding outwards from its primordial origin, corroborating the majestic wording of revelation.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Qurtubi (17/48) • Mafatih al-Ghayb (Al-Razi) • Mufradat Alfaz al-Qur'an

Your Sustenance is in the Heavens: Rizqukum fees-Sama' (51:22–23)

Allah swears by the Lord of the heavens and earth that human provision is decreed in the celestial realm: 'And in the sky is your sustenance and whatever you are promised. So by the Lord of the heaven and the earth, it is true, just as you are able to speak!'

When Al-Asma'i recited this verse to a desert nomad, the Bedouin wept in awe, exclaiming: 'Who angered the Almighty so much that He had to take an oath to make mortals believe their food is guaranteed?!' It permanently breaks the shackles of subservience to oppressive bosses or tyrants for livelihood.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/225) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/417) • Ihya' 'Uloom al-Deen (Al-Ghazali)

Sacred Abrahamic Hospitality: The Fat Roasted Calf (51:24–28)

When unfamiliar travelers entered his dwelling, Ibrahim (AS) did not interrogate their identities or hesitate. He slipped away immediately, slaughtered his best livestock, and served a roasted fat calf (*'Ijlun Haneed*) before asking a single question.

Scholars extract twelve ethical rules of hospitality from these verses: returning a greeting with something better, serving guests swiftly, bringing the best food available, placing food directly before them rather than commanding them to fetch it, and speaking with exquisite gentleness.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/418) • Zad al-Ma'ad (Ibn al-Qayyim)
