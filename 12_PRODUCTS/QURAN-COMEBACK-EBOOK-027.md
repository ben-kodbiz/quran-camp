# CHAPTER 27: SURAH AN-NAML (سُورَةُ النمل)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 27  
**Total Verses:** 93  
**Famous Names:** Surat an-Naml, Surat Sulayman  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah An-Naml is the Qur'anic masterpiece of wisdom, divine signs, and kingdom governance. It is renowned for containing the Basmalah twice (in verse 1 and inside Sulayman's royal diplomatic letter in verse 30: *Innahu min Sulaymana wa-innahu Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim*). It reveals the astonishing speech of non-human creatures: a tiny female ant coordinating her colony to avoid being crushed by Sulayman's army, and the hoopoe (*Hudhud*) bird operating as an investigative reporter uncovering the sun-worshipping empire of the Queen of Sheba (Bilqis). Despite holding unmatched power and wealth, Sulayman remains profoundly humble, weeping in gratitude: 'This is from the favor of my Lord to test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful' (27:40).

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in Makkah to reassure the persecuted believers that real power belongs to the Lord of the worlds. It illustrates how true righteous leaders handle immense political authority, wealth, and technological dominion—not through tyranny, but through gratitude, justice, and devotion to Tawhid.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**Divine Omniscience, the Humility of True Power, Creation's Praise, and Gratitude as the Highest Response to Worldly Blessing.**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### The Ant's Compassionate Warning (27:18–19)
$$\text{حَتَّىٰ إِذَا أَتَوْا عَلَىٰ وَادِ النَّمْلِ قَالَتْ نَمْلَةٌ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّمْلُ ادْخُلُوا مَسَاكِنَكُمْ لَا يَحْطِمَنَّكُمْ سُلَيْمَانُ وَجُنُودُهُ وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ ۝ فَتَبَسَّمَ ضَاحِكًا مِّن قَوْلِهَا وَقَالَ رَبِّ أَوْزِعْنِي أَنْ أَشْكُرَ نِعْمَتَكَ الَّتِي أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيَّ وَعَلَىٰ وَالِدَيَّ وَأَنْ أَعْمَلَ صَالِحًا تَرْضَاهُ وَأَدْخِلْنِي بِرَحْمَتِكَ فِي عِبَادِكَ الصَّالِحِينَ}$$
*"Until, when they came upon the valley of the ants, an ant said, 'O ants, enter your dwellings that you not be crushed by Solomon and his soldiers while they perceive not.' So [Solomon] smiled, amused at her speech, and said, 'My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor which You have bestowed upon me and upon my parents and to do righteousness of which You approve. And admit me by Your mercy into [the ranks of] Your righteous servants.'"*

### Sulayman's Diplomatic Letter (27:30–31)
$$\text{إِنَّهُ مِن سُلَيْمَانَ وَإِنَّهُ بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ ۝ أَلَّا تَعْلُوا عَلَيَّ وَأْتُونِي مُسْلِمِينَ}$$
*"Indeed, it is from Solomon, and indeed, it reads: 'In the name of Allah, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful, Be not haughty with me but come to me in submission [as Muslims].'"*

### Testing Through Abundance (27:40)
$$\text{قَالَ هَٰذَا مِن فَضْلِ رَبِّي لِيَبْلُوَنِي أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ ۖ وَمَن شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِ ۖ وَمَن كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيٌّ كَرِيمٌ}$$
*"He said, 'This is from the favor of my Lord to test me whether I will be grateful or ungrateful. And whoever is grateful is grateful for [the benefit of] himself. And whoever is ungrateful—then indeed, my Lord is Free of need and Generous.'"*

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *27:59:* 'Say, [O Muhammad], "Praise be to Allah, and peace upon His servants whom He has chosen. Is Allah better or that which they associate with Him?"'
* *27:62:* 'Is not He [better] who responds to the desperate one when he calls upon Him and removes evil and makes you inheritors of the earth? Is there a deity with Allah? Little do you remember.'
* *27:88:* 'And you see the mountains, thinking them rigid, while they will pass as the passing of clouds. [It is] the work of Allah, who perfected all things...'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Ant's Courtesy and Apology (Verse 18):** Scholars of Tafsir, including Ibn Kathir and Al-Qurtubi, marvel at the eloquence of the ant: She addressed her entire community (*Ya ayyuhan-naml*), issued a command (*udkhulu*), gave the reason (*la yahtimannakum*), identified the leader (*Sulayman*), and remarkably offered an excuse for the soldiers (*wa hum la yash'urun*—'while they do not realize'). Even an ant gave the benefit of the doubt to human beings.
* **The Dua of Prophetic Gratitude (Verse 19):** Sulayman did not boast of his military power or animal communication; he smiled and immediately made du'a: *Rabbi awzi'ni an ashkura ni'mataka* ('Enable me to thank You'). He recognized that gratitude (*Shukr*) is not an automatic human achievement, but a divine gift that must be divinely enabled.

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **The Prohibition of Killing Ants:**  
   *Sunan Abi Dawud (Hadith 5267), Sahih:*  
   "Ibn Abbas (RA) narrated: 'The Prophet ﷺ forbade the killing of four creatures: the ant, the honeybee, the hoopoe (*Hudhud*), and the shrike (*Surad*).'"
2. **The Du'a of the Desperate:**  
   *Musnad Ahmad (Hadith 20433), Sahih:*  
   "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said regarding verse 27:62: 'The supplication of the distressed (*Amman yujibul-mudtarra idha da'ah*) is answered, for Allah removes the evil and grants deliverance.'"
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Shukr an-Ni'mah (Gratitude for Favor):** Recognizing that every talent, title, and blessing is a divine test of loyalty, not personal entitlement.
2. **Husn az-Zann (Giving Benefit of the Doubt):** The noble moral instinct, modeled even by the ant, to assume good intentions rather than malice.
3. **Mulkiyyah bil-'Adl (Just Governance):** Using power, intellect, and authority strictly to uplift truth and guide souls to monotheism.
4. **Tasbih al-Kawn (Universal Glorification):** The cosmic reality that all animals, birds, and insects worship and glorify Allah in ways humans cannot comprehend.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **The Leadership of the Tiny Ant (27:18):** Consider this creature: She did not say, 'Someone else will warn them,' or 'I am just an ant, what can I do?' She saw danger approaching her community and immediately raised the alarm, saving thousands. Never underestimate your capacity to protect, warn, and guide others, no matter how small your platform or title.
* **Haza Min Fadli Rabbi (27:40):** When you get the promotion, close the massive contract, or achieve the academic honor, what is your first thought? Is it, 'I worked so hard for this, I am so smart'? Sulayman saw a royal throne transported across hundreds of miles in the blink of an eye, and his immediate response was: *Haza min fadli Rabbi li-yabluwani a-ashkuru am akfur.* Every success is a test question: Will you thank Him, or will you grow proud?
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* Whenever I achieve a major milestone, I utter *Haza min fadli Rabbi* and dedicate a portion of my resources to charity in gratitude.
* I learn from the ant to step up and serve my family and community with proactive responsibility without waiting for formal authority.
* I cultivate reverence for all of Allah's creation, avoiding cruelty or careless destruction of nature and living things.

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Recite Sulayman's Gratitude Du'a (27:19) Daily:**  
Memorize: *Rabbi awzi'ni an ashkura ni'matakallati an'amta 'alayya wa 'ala walidayya wa an a'mala salihan tardahu wa adkhilni bi-rahmatika fi 'ibadikas-salihin.* Recite it once every morning after Fajr.

---

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. Do I view my wealth, talents, and career as personal entitlements or as rigorous divine tests of gratitude (27:40)?
2. Like the ant who gave Sulayman's army the benefit of the doubt (27:18), do I offer generous excuses for people's oversights?
3. When I face an insoluble dilemma, do I call upon Allah as the desperate one (*Al-Mudtarr*) described in verse 62?
