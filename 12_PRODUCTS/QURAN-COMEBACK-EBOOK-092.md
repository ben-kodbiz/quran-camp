# CHAPTER 92: SURAH AL-LAYL (سُورَةُ الليل)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 92  
**Total Verses:** 21  
**Famous Names:** Surat al-Layl, Wal-Layli Idha Yaghsha  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah Al-Layl is the definitive behavioral blueprint of two opposite human trajectories. Opening with oaths by the night as it covers, the day as it shines, and the creation of male and female, it establishes: 'Indeed, your efforts are diverse' (*Inna sa'yakum lashatta*). It maps human destiny into two paths: Path One: the one who gives charity, maintains God-consciousness (*Ittaqa*), and affirms the ultimate good (*Saddaqa bil-Husna*)—for him, Allah eases the path to ease (*Fasanuyassiruhu lil-yusra*). Path Two: the one who hoards wealth, acts self-sufficient (*Istaghna*), and denies the ultimate good—for him, Allah eases the path to hardship (*Fasanuyassiruhu lil-'usra*). It culminates in the portrait of Abu Bakr as-Siddiq: the most God-fearing servant who spends his wealth purely to purify himself, seeking only the Face of his Lord, Most High: 'And he will surely be well-pleased' (92:21).

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed in Makkah regarding Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (RA), who spent his personal fortune purchasing and emancipating tortured enslaved Muslims (like Bilal and 'Amir ibn Fuhayrah). When his father Abu Quhafah told him to buy strong young men for physical protection instead, Surah Al-Layl descended to validate Abu Bakr's pure intentions.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**Diverse Human Strivings (*Sa'yun Shatta*), the Equation of Ease vs. Hardship, Sincere Philanthropy, and the Eternal Honor of Abu Bakr as-Siddiq.**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### Diverse Strivings and the Path of Ease (92:4–7)
$$\text{إِنَّ سَعْيَكُمْ لَشَتَّىٰ ۝ فَأَمَّا مَنْ أَعْطَىٰ وَاتَّقَىٰ ۝ وَصَدَّقَ بِالْحُسْنَىٰ ۝ فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِلْيُسْرَىٰ}$$
*"Indeed, your efforts are diverse. As for he who gives and fears Allah, And believes in the best [reward], We will ease him toward ease."*

### The Path of Hardship (92:8–10)
$$\text{وَأَمَّا مَن بَخِلَ وَاسْتَغْنَىٰ ۝ وَكَذَّبَ بِالْحُسْنَىٰ ۝ فَسَنُيَسِّرُهُ لِلْعُسْرَىٰ}$$
*"But as for he who withholds and considers himself free of need, And denies the best [reward], We will ease him toward hardship."*

### The Portrait of Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (92:17–21)
$$\text{وَسَيُجَنَّبُهَا الْأَتْقَى ۝ الَّذِي يُؤْتِي مَالَهُ يَتَزَكَّىٰ ۝ وَمَا لِأَحَدٍ عِندَهُ مِن نِّعْمَةٍ تُجْزَىٰ ۝ إِلَّا ابْتِغَاءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِ الْأَعْلَىٰ ۝ وَلَسَوْفَ يَرْضَىٰ}$$
*"But the righteous one will avoid it—[He] who gives from his wealth to purify himself, And not [giving any benefit] for which he is to be repaid, But only seeking the countenance of his Lord, Most High. And he will surely be well-pleased."*

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *92:11:* 'And what will his wealth avail him when he falls?'
* *92:12–13:* 'Indeed, upon Us is guidance, And indeed, to Us belongs the Hereafter and the first [life].'
* *92:14:* 'So I have warned you of a Fire which is blazing.'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Equation of Ease (*Fasanuyassiruhu lil-Yusra*) (Verses 5–7):** Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA) and Imam Ibn Kathir explain: When a person takes the first three steps—(1) Giving generously (*A'ta*), (2) Living with moral conscience (*Ittaqa*), and (3) Believing with certainty in Jannah (*Saddaqa bil-Husna*)—Allah opens a supernatural ease for him. Good deeds become beloved and easy, prayer becomes a delight, and sinful impulses lose their grip on him.
* **The Supreme Philanthropy of Abu Bakr (Verses 17–21):** All classical mufassirun agree that *Al-Atqa* (the most pious) refers specifically to Abu Bakr (RA). He spent immense fortunes buying tortured slaves, not because they owed him a favor or could ever repay him (*Wa ma li-ahadin 'indahu min ni'matin tujza*), but solely seeking the Face of his Lord, Most High (*Ibtigha'a wajhi Rabbihil-A'la*). And Allah promised him: *Wa lasawfa yarda* ('And he will surely be satisfied!').
---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **Every Person's Path is Made Easy:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 4949) from Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA):*  
   "We were sitting with the Prophet ﷺ in Baqi' al-Gharqad. He tapped the ground with a stick and said: 'There is none among you except that his place in Paradise or Hell has been written.' The Companions asked: 'O Messenger of Allah, should we not then depend on our decree and abandon striving?' The Prophet ﷺ said: *'No! Strive and work! For everyone will be eased toward that for which he was created.'* Then he recited: *Fa-amma man a'ta wattaqa, wa saddaqa bil-husna, fasanuyassiruhu lil-yusra...* (92:5–10)."
2. **Abu Bakr's Charity:**  
   *Sunan Abi Dawud (Hadith 1678), Sahih:*  
   "Umar ibn al-Khattab said: 'The Messenger of Allah ﷺ commanded us to give charity. I brought half of my wealth and thought: Today I will surpass Abu Bakr! But Abu Bakr arrived with his entire wealth. The Prophet ﷺ asked: *What did you leave for your family?* Abu Bakr replied: *I left for them Allah and His Messenger.*'"
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Inna Sa'yakum Lashatta (Divergent Strivings):** The reality that humanity is split into fundamentally contrasting moral trajectories.
2. **At-Taysir lil-Yusra (Divine Facilitation of Good):** The spiritual phenomenon where sincere good deeds unlock ease, clarity, and joy in continuous obedience.
3. **At-Taysir lil-'Usra (Facilitation of Hardship):** The terrible spiritual blindness where persistent greed and denial make sin feel attractive and virtue feel unbearable.
4. **Ibtigha' Wajh Allah (Seeking the Divine Countenance):** The pinnacle of sincerity: giving wealth without expectation of repayment, praise, or social leverage.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **Which Path Are You Being Eased Toward? (92:7 vs 92:10):** Look at your daily life: When it's time for prayer, does your heart leap up easily (*Nuyassiruhu lil-yusra*), or does your body feel like lead dragging itself to the mat? When you give charity, does your hand open joyfully, or do your fingers cling to your wallet? If good deeds feel easy and sins feel repulsive, thank Allah: you are on the path to *Al-Yusra*. If prayer feels heavy and sin feels effortless, run to Allah in repentance before you are locked into *Al-'Usra*.
* **Give Like Abu Bakr: Zero Strings Attached (92:19–20):** When you help someone—a relative, a friend, an employee—do you subtly expect them to vote for you, invite you, compliment you, or treat you with deference? Abu Bakr spent his fortune buying Bilal and helpless slaves who had zero social capital. He expected *nothing* from creation: *Illa-btigha'a wajhi Rabbihil-A'la.* Cleanse your charity of all strings attached.
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* I cultivate the three keys to ease: giving generously (*A'ta*), guarding my conscience (*Ittaqa*), and believing in the ultimate reward (92:5–7).
* I eradicate stinginess and self-sufficiency (*Bukhl* and *Istighna*), fearing the slippery path to hardship (92:8–10).
* I give charity purely for the Face of Allah, expecting neither favors nor repayment from anyone (92:19–20).

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Give a 'Strings-Free' Gift Today:**  
Follow the model of Abu Bakr (92:19): Give a financial gift, service, or meal today to someone who cannot possibly repay you or advance your worldly interests, doing it solely for the Face of Allah.

---

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. Are my daily habits leading me toward *Al-Yusra* (spiritual ease) or *Al-'Usra* (hardship) (92:7 vs 92:10)?
2. Do I attach subtle worldly expectations to my charity and favors for people (92:19)?
3. How does the promise *Wa lasawfa yarda* ('He will surely be well-pleased') inspire me to sacrifice for Allah (92:21)?

## 13. Closing Supplication
$$\text{اللَّهُمَّ يَسِّرْنَا لِلْيُسْرَىٰ وَجَنِّبْنَا الْعُسْرَىٰ ، اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ أَعْطَىٰ وَاتَّقَىٰ وَصَدَّقَ بِالْحُسْنَىٰ ، وَلَا تَجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ بَخِلَ وَاسْتَغْنَىٰ ، وَارْزُقْنَا إِخْلَاصًا كَإِخْلَاصِ أَبِي بَكْرٍ الصِّدِّيقِ}$$  
*"O Allah, ease us toward ease and preserve us from hardship. O Allah, make us among those who give, fear You, and believe in the best reward. Do not make us among those who withhold and deem themselves self-sufficient, and bless us with sincerity like that of Abu Bakr as-Siddiq. Amin."*
