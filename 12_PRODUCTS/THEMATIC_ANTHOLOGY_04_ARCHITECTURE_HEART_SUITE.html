Why the Righteous Disdained Human Gratitude in Surah Al-Insan

The Concept of Qalb Saleem in Sunni Exegesis

In Surah Ash-Shu'ara (26:88-89), Prophet Ibrahim proclaims: 'Yawma la yanfa'u malun wa la banoon, illa man atallaha bi-qalbin saleem' (The Day on which neither wealth nor children will avail, except one who comes to Allah with a sound heart). In Arabic lexicography, 'saleem' denotes that which is healthy, intact, free from defect, and completely surrendered.

Imam Ibn Kathir and Al-Qurtubi explain that a 'sound heart' possesses two essential characteristics: first, it is cleansed of shirk (polytheism), doubt, and innovation; second, it is purified of moral diseases such as malice, envy, arrogance, and worldly greed.

Imam Ibn al-Qayyim remarks in Madarij as-Salikeen that the heart is the sovereign king of the human organism, while the limbs are its soldiers. If the king is sound and submitted to God, the soldiers act in justice; but if the heart is diseased, the entire life collapses into spiritual ruin.

Authorities: Tafsir Ibn Kathir 26:89, Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Ihyah 'Ulum ad-Deen (Al-Ghazali)

The Psychology of Dhikr & Itmi'nan: The Settling of the Soul

In Surah Ar-Ra'd (13:28), the Qur'an articulates the foundational principle of spiritual psychology: 'Ala bi-dhikrillahi tatma'innul-quloob' (Verily, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest). The intensive particle 'ala' signals an awakened proclamation, while 'tatma'in' signifies a heart settling into profound, unwavering tranquility after disturbance.

Fakhr ad-Din ar-Razi observes that the human heart was created with an innate existential longing for the Infinite. Therefore, so long as it seeks satisfaction in finite worldly entities—wealth, physical beauty, political rank, fame—it experiences perpetual anxiety, because all created things are fragile and mortal.

Only when the heart anchors its remembrance in the Ever-Living, Infinite Creator does it reach true equilibrium (itmi'nan), emancipated from the dread of loss and the torment of existential emptiness.

Authorities: Tafsir ar-Razi 13:28, Tafsir al-Qurtubi, Al-Wabil as-Sayyib (Ibn al-Qayyim)

The Spiritual Pathology of Hardened Hearts (Al-Hadid 57:16)

In Surah Al-Hadid (57:16), Allah addresses the believers with tender urgency: 'Alam ya'ni lilladheena amanoo an takhsha'a quloobuhum li-dhikrillah' (Has the time not arrived for those who have believed that their hearts should soften in humility to the remembrance of Allah?).

In Sahih Muslim (3027), Abdullah ibn Mas'ud testified: 'Between our acceptance of Islam and the revelation of this verse, with which Allah admonished us, there were only four years.' The companions were the elite of humanity; yet Allah noticed that the passage of time without intense vigilance can lead even sincere hearts into spiritual relaxation and gradual callousness.

The verse warns: 'Wa la yakoonoo kalladheena ootul-kitaba min qablu fa-tala 'alayhimul-amadu fa-qasat quloobuhum' (Do not be like those given Scripture before, over whom a long time passed, so their hearts grew hard). Spiritual vitality requires regular, deliberate contemplation (tadabbur) to prevent the creeping rust of heedlessness.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 3027, Tafsir Ibn Kathir 57:16, Kitab az-Zuhd (Ahmad ibn Hanbal)

The Sovereignty of Sincerity (Ikhlas) over External Form

Surah Al-Insan (76:8-9) depicts the apex of pure intentionality: the righteous feeding the destitute, orphan, and prisoner, declaring inwardly: 'Innama nut'imukum li-wajhillahi la nureedu minkum jazaa'an wa la shukoora' (We feed you only for the Face of Allah; we desire from you neither reward nor thanks).

Imam Al-Qurtubi notes that they did not even utter these words out loud; Allah knew their secret internal intentionality and praised them in Scripture for humanity to recite until the end of time. They disdained even verbal gratitude ('shukoor') because true sincerity seeks zero return from created beings.

In Sunni theology, intentionality (niyyah) transfigures the ordinary into the sacred. A simple meal given with pure ikhlas ascends above the heavens, while vast fortunes donated for social acclaim or public vanity are reduced to worthless dust on the Day of Reckoning.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Qurtubi 76:9, Jami' al-Ulum wal-Hikam (Ibn Rajab), Fath al-Bari
