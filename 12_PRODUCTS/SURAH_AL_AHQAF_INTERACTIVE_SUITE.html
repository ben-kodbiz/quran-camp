The Delegation of Jinn at Batn Nakhlah (46:29–32)

In Sahih Muslim (449) and Tafsir Ibn Kathir, Ibn Abbas and Ibn Mas'ud record that after the Prophet ﷺ was rejected and stoned at Ta'if, he stood in prayer at Batn Nakhlah during the night. A cohort of seven to nine jinn from Nisibin passed by.

Hearing the recitation, they hushed one another in solemn awe: 'Ansitool' (Listen in silence!). Deeply moved, they returned immediately to their communities as passionate monotheistic warners, testifying that this revelation corroborated the Torah of Moses.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 449 • Sahih al-Bukhari • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/298) • Al-Wahidi p. 278

The Fortieth Year Supplication & Filial Piety (46:15)

Classical exegetes, including Al-Baghawi and Ibn Kathir, note that this verse highlights Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (RA), who embraced Islam and prayed for his parents and children until his entire family line entered the fold of faith.

At forty, physical vigor meets reflective wisdom. The verse establishes the foundational duty of Ihsan toward parents, acknowledging the thirty months of maternal hardship, and teaches the comprehensive prayer for righteous lineage.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/105) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/293) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Al-Wahidi)

The Sand Dunes of Al-Ahqaf & The Cloud of Doom (46:21–25)

Hud (AS) warned the mighty empire of 'Ad in the southern Arabian desert dunes (Al-Ahqaf). Blessed with towering stature and fortress dwellings, they boasted: 'Who is mightier than us in strength?'

When drought struck, they saw a dark cloud expanding across the horizon and rejoiced: 'This cloud brings us rain!' Hud warned: 'Nay, it is that which you asked to hasten: a furious hurricane bearing agonizing doom!' It raged for seven nights and eight days until nothing was left except their vacant abodes.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4829 • Sahih Muslim 899 • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (16/205)

The Resolute Arch-Messengers: Ulu al-'Azm (46:35)

Surah Al-Ahqaf closes the seven Hawameem by directing the Prophet ﷺ to emulate the supreme patience of the Ulu al-'Azm—the five resolute pillars of revelation: Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa, and Muhammad ﷺ.

They faced exile, fire, oceans, persecution, and military coalitions, yet never compromised their divine trust or cursed their people in haste. The verse cements steadfast patience (Sabr) as the ultimate virtue of prophetic leadership.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/124) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/305) • Sharh Muslim (An-Nawawi)
