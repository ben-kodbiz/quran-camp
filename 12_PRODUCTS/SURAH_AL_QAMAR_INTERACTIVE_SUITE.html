The Miracle of the Splitting of the Moon: Inshaqa al-Qamar (54:1–2)

In Sahih al-Bukhari (3636, 4864) and Sahih Muslim (2800), Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, Anas ibn Malik, and Ibn Abbas narrate that the Meccan polytheists challenged the Prophet ﷺ: 'If you are truly a prophet, split the moon for us into two halves.'

The Prophet supplicated, and Allah split the moon into two distinct pieces, one hovering above Mount Abu Qubays and the other behind it, with Mount Hira visible between them. The Prophet cried out: 'Bear witness! Bear witness!' Yet pagan chieftains turned away, dismissing it as 'passing sorcery' (*Sihrun Mustamirr*).

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 3636, 4864 • Sahih Muslim 2800 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/474) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Al-Wahidi)

The Fourfold Refrain of Accessibility: Wa Laqad Yassarna al-Qur'an (54:17, 22, 32, 40)

Four separate times in Surah Al-Qamar, following the destruction of Noah's flood, 'Ad's hurricane, Thamud's sonic blast, and Pharaoh's drowning, Allah repeats this exact refrain: 'And We have certainly made the Qur'an easy for remembrance—so is there anyone who will remember?'

Classical scholars note that this repetition serves as a structural refrain proving that unlike previous scriptures, Allah has facilitated the Qur'an for memorization (Hifdh), recitation (Tilawah), and contemplation (Tadabbur), leaving humanity without excuse for heedlessness.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/294) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/478) • Al-Burhan (Az-Zarkashi)

The Prophecy of Badr Foretold in Makkah: Sa-yuhzamu al-Jam' (54:45)

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4875), Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) narrated: 'When this verse was revealed in Makkah: *The coalition will soon be routed and turn their backs fleeing*, I thought to myself: Which coalition? We are few and hunted down! But on the day of the Battle of Badr, I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ donning his armor and declaring: *Sa-yuhzamu al-jam'u wa yuwalloona ad-dubur!* Then I understood the miraculous reality of the verse.'

It stands as undeniable proof of the prophetic foreknowledge granted through revelation.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4875 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/485) • As-Seerah an-Nabawiyyah (Ibn Hisham)

The Seat of Sincerity with the Sovereign King: Maq'ad Sidq (54:55)

Surah Al-Qamar closes with an extraordinary verse describing the final destination of the righteous: 'In an assembly of truth and sincerity (*Maq'ad Sidq*), in the presence of an All-Powerful Sovereign King (*'Inda Maleekin Muqtadir*).'

Ibn al-Qayyim remarks that the phrase *Maq'ad Sidq* signifies a station free from all pretense, falsehood, transience, and fear. Being *'Inda* (in the intimate company) of the Almighty King represents the absolute climax of human spiritual attainment.

Authorities: Madarij as-Salikin (Ibn al-Qayyim) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (17/152) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/488)
