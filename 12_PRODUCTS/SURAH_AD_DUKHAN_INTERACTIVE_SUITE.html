The Blessed Night & Apportionment of Destiny (44:1–4)

Surah Ad-Dukhan reveals that the Qur'an descended in a Blessed Night (*Fee laylatin mubarakah*), identified by Ibn Abbas and Sunni consensus as Laylat al-Qadr.

In this night, every matter of wise divine governance is distinctly resolved and transferred to the archangels: the decrees of lifespans, sustenance, trials, and births for the forthcoming year.

Authorities: Tafsir al-Tabari (22/9) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/245) • Sahih al-Bukhari

The Visible Smoke & The Famine of Makkah (44:10–16)

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4809) and Sahih Muslim (2798), Abdullah ibn Mas'ud relates that when Quraysh proved obstinate, the Prophet ﷺ prayed for a drought like that of Yusuf. Famine struck so severely that men looked at the sky and saw only what resembled smoke.

Abu Sufyan begged the Prophet to pray for rain, promising they would believe. Rain fell, yet they relapsed into idolatry. Allah announced: 'On the Day We will strike them with the supreme strike (*Al-Batshata al-Kubra*)'—which was fulfilled at the Battle of Badr.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4809 • Sahih Muslim 2798 • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (16/128)

Neither Heaven Nor Earth Wept for Them (44:29)

When Pharaoh and his armies were drowned, Allah stated: 'Neither the heaven nor the earth wept over them.' Ibn Abbas explained that when a righteous believer dies, the place on earth where he prayed and the portal in heaven through which his good deeds ascended weep for his loss.

For arrogant tyrants who left behind only bloodshed and monuments of oppression, not a single star or grain of sand mourns their departure.

Authorities: Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3255 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/253) • Tafsir al-Tabari

The Tree of Zaqqum & The Secure Abode (44:43–51)

Surah Ad-Dukhan presents an eschatological study in contrasts: for the persistent oppressor, the Tree of Zaqqum boils in his intestines like molten metal (*Kal-Muhl*), while he is dragged to the center of the Fire.

For the God-conscious (*Al-Muttaqoon*), they reside in a station of absolute security (*Maqamin Ameen*), draped in silk and brocade, paired with pure spouses, and granted immortal life untouched by fear or death.

Authorities: Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/257) • Tafsir al-Baghawi • Madarij as-Salikin
