The Verification Mandate: In Ja'akum Fasiq (49:6)

In classical Hadith literature and Tafsir Ibn Kathir, this verse was revealed concerning Al-Walid ibn 'Uqbah, who was sent by the Prophet ﷺ to collect Zakat from Banu al-Mustaliq. Hearing rumors of hostility, he returned claiming they had apostatized and mobilized for war.

Before ordering a retaliatory detachment, the Prophet ﷺ investigated through Khalid ibn al-Walid, discovering Banu al-Mustaliq were actively praying and eagerly awaiting to pay Zakat. Allah revealed: 'O you who believe! If a corrupt person brings you news, verify it thoroughly (Fatabayyanoo), lest you harm people in ignorance and become stricken with remorse!'

Authorities: Musnad Ahmad • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/370) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Al-Wahidi p. 280) • Al-Tabari

The Universal Human Equality Charter (49:13)

At the Conquest of Makkah, when Bilal ibn Rabah (the formerly enslaved Abyssinian) ascended the roof of the Ka'bah to proclaim the Adhan, several pagan aristocrats sneered with racist remarks.

In response, Allah revealed this historic declaration addressed to all humanity: 'O mankind! We created you from a single male and female, and made you into nations and tribes that you may know one another (Li-Ta'arafoo). Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most God-conscious!' In his Farewell Sermon, the Prophet ﷺ immortalized this: 'No Arab has superiority over a non-Arab, nor a white person over a black person, except by Taqwa.'

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari • Sunan al-Bayhaqi • Musnad Ahmad • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (7/385)

The Psychological Parable of Backbiting: Gheebah (49:12)

Surah Al-Hujurat constructs the most terrifying and visceral psychological deterrent against character assassination: equating backbiting (Gheebah) with necrophagy—eating the raw, decaying corpse of one's own dead brother.

The Prophet ﷺ defined Gheebah in Sahih Muslim: 'Mentioning your brother with that which he would dislike.' When asked, 'What if what I say is true?' he replied: 'If it is true, you have backbitten him; and if it is false, you have slandered him (Buhtan).' It forbids all forms of malicious gossip, digital doxxing, and secret fault-hunting.

Authorities: Sahih Muslim 2589 • Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1934 • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (16/335)

Social Decorum & Calling Behind Apartments (49:2–5)

Revealed regarding a delegation from Banu Tamim who arrived in Medina at midday during the Prophet's resting hour, shouting loudly from outside his private rooms: 'O Muhammad, come out to us!'

The verses codified refined social manners: respecting privacy, not shouting over others, and avoiding disruptive entitlement. In Sahih al-Bukhari (4846), Thabit ibn Qays (who naturally had a loud voice) wept in fear that his deeds were destroyed, until the Prophet comforted him with glad tidings of Paradise.

Authorities: Sahih al-Bukhari 4845, 4846 • Sahih Muslim • Tafsir al-Tabari (22/195)
