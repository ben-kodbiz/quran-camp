# CHAPTER 97: SURAH AL-QADR (سُورَةُ القدر)
**Classification:** Makki  
**Order in Mushaf:** 97  
**Total Verses:** 5  
**Famous Names:** Surat al-Qadr, Inna Anzalnahu  

---

## 1. Why This Surah Matters
Surah Al-Qadr unveils the most sacred nocturnal moment in human history: the Night of Decree (*Laylat al-Qadr*). In five jewel-like verses, Allah reveals that one single night of sincere devotion is superior to a thousand months—an entire human lifetime of over eighty-three years. On this night, the Qur'an was sent down as a complete unit from the Preserved Tablet (*Al-Lawh al-Mahfuz*) to the lowest heaven (*Bayt al-'Izzah*), and hosts of angels, commanded by Jibril (*Ar-Ruh*), descend in radiant processions with every divine decree. The night is enveloped in unbroken spiritual peace (*Salam*) until the first rays of dawn.

---

## 2. Surah Context
Revealed to console and honor the Ummah of Muhammad ﷺ, whose physical lifespans are shorter than earlier nations, by gifting them an annual opportunity to surpass generations of worship in a single night.

---

## 3. Central Theme
**The Sublime Stature of Laylat al-Qadr, the Heavenly Descent of the Qur'an and Angels, and Divine Peace Encompassing Believers.**

---

## 4. Key Passages
### The Descent in the Night of Decree (97:1–3)
$$\text{إِنَّا أَنزَلْنَاهُ فِي لَيْلَةِ الْقَدْرِ ۝ وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ ۝ لَيْلَةُ الْقَدْرِ خَيْرٌ مِّنْ أَلْفِ شَهْرٍ}$$
*"Indeed, We sent the Qur'an down during the Night of Decree. And what can make you know what is the Night of Decree? The Night of Decree is better than a thousand months."*

### The Descent of the Angels and the Holy Spirit (97:4–5)
$$\text{تَنَزَّلُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ وَالرُّوحُ فِيهَا بِإِذْنِ رَبِّهِم مِّن كُلِّ أَمْرٍ ۝ سَلَامٌ هِيَ حَتَّىٰ مَطْلَعِ الْفَجْرِ}$$
*"The angels and the Spirit descend therein by permission of their Lord for every matter. Peace it is until the emergence of dawn."*

---

## 5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)
* *97:1:* 'Indeed, We sent it down on the Night of Destiny (*Al-Qadr*).'
* *97:3:* 'The Night of Glory is better than a thousand months.'
* *97:5:* 'It is all peace until the break of dawn (*Matla'il Fajr*).'

---

## 6. What Classical Tafsir Explains
* **The Multiple Meanings of 'Qadr':** Classical scholars including Ibn Kathir and Az-Zajjaj note that *Qadr* has three simultaneous dimensions: (1) Honor and High Stature (*Sharaf*), because whoever worships in it gains immense nobility; (2) Decree and Destiny (*Taqdeer*), because the annual measures of life, death, and provision are written down for the angels; and (3) Constriction (*Diq*), because the earth becomes packed tight with descending angelic multitudes.
* **Better Than a Thousand Months:** Imam As-Sa'di writes that good deeds performed on this single night—prayers, charity, recitation, and repentance—weigh more heavily on the divine scale than equivalent deeds performed over 1,000 months (83 years and 4 months) that do not contain Laylat al-Qadr.
* **The Spirit (*Ar-Ruh*):** Scholars unanimously agree that *Ar-Ruh* is Jibril (Gabriel) AS, singled out after the general mention of angels to showcase his preeminent status as the conveyor of revelation.
---

## 7. Important Supporting Hadiths
1. **Seeking Laylat al-Qadr in the Odd Nights:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 2017) from 'A'ishah (RA):*  
   "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'Search for the Night of Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten nights of Ramadan.'"
2. **Forgiveness of All Past Sins:**  
   *Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 1901) & Sahih Muslim (Hadith 760) from Abu Hurayrah (RA):*  
   "The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Whoever stands in prayer on Laylat al-Qadr out of faith and seeking reward, all his previous sins will be forgiven.'"
3. **The Supplication of the Night:**  
   *Jami' at-Tirmidhi (Hadith 3513), Sahih:*  
   "'A'ishah (RA) asked: 'O Messenger of Allah, if I know which night is Laylat al-Qadr, what should I say?' He said: 'Say: *Allahumma innaka 'Afuwwun tuhibbul 'afwa fa'fu 'anni* (O Allah, You are Most Forgiving and You love forgiveness, so forgive me).'"
---

## 8. Key Concepts
1. **Laylat al-Qadr (The Night of Decree):** The paramount night of spiritual acceleration and annual decree in Ramadan.
2. **Ar-Ruh (The Holy Spirit / Jibril):** The archangel who brings divine light and comfort to believers.
3. **Salam (Transcendent Peace):** A night shielded from evil, filled with angelic blessings and forgiveness until dawn.
4. **Al-Husna (Multiplied Grace):** Divine mercy compensating for short human lifespans through accelerated spiritual reward.
---

## 9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)
* **The Value of a Single Night:** Contemplate: How much effort do we exert for decades of career promotion, versus one night of genuine, heartfelt tears before our Lord that redefines our eternal destiny?
* **Angels Walking Amongst Us:** Imagine the atmosphere of that night: the earth crowded with pure celestial beings who say 'Ameen' to every sincere prayer of a broken heart.
* **Living in Salam:** Reflect on the closing word *Salam*: cultivate a heart that is completely at peace with Allah's decrees, free from malice toward His creation.
---

## 10. What This Means For Me
* My life is too short to neglect the last ten nights of Ramadan; Laylat al-Qadr is a priceless gift I cannot afford to lose.
* Allah's forgiveness is greater than my mountain of mistakes; one night of pure *Tawbah* can wipe my slate clean forever.
* Peace is not found in outward circumstances; true peace is descending from the heavens right now through devotion to the Qur'an.

---

## 11. One Practical Action
**Memorize and Internalize the Qadr Dua:**  
Memorize the supplication: *Allahumma innaka 'Afuwwun tuhibbul 'afwa fa'fu 'anni*. Recite it daily with deep contemplation of Allah's love for wiping out sins completely, and plan your schedule to maximize the last ten nights of upcoming Ramadan.

## 12. Reflection Questions
1. How does knowing that one night equals 83 years of worship change your perception of time and divine mercy?
2. What bad habits or grievances must you let go of to welcome the divine peace (*Salam*) described in verse 5?
3. How can you prepare weeks in advance so that you do not face Laylat al-Qadr exhausted or distracted?
