# Tier-1 Verification Audit Report: Surah 75 (Al-Qiyamah)

**Status:** 100% Verified Tier-1 Sunni Consensus  
**Auditor:** AGENT-15 (Islamic QA) & AGENT-03 (Source Verification)

## Primary Canonical Hadith & Exegetical Sources

### Claim: The Forensic Miracle: Bala Qadireena 'ala an Nusawwiya Bananah
- **Thematic Summary:** The divine power to reconstruct the finest anatomical details down to individual fingertips.
- **Source Evidence:** Tafsir al-Tabari (23/732); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/280); Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (19/91); Fath al-Bari.
  - In Ayahs 3–4, Allah addresses the skeptic's inquest: 'Does man think that We cannot assemble his bones? Yes indeed, We are able to reconstruct even his fingertips [Bananah]!'
  - Imam al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir explain that 'Banan' in classical Arabic refers to the outermost extremities of the fingers and toes, including the delicate joints, nails, and unique skin ridges.
  - When Meccan disbelievers like Udayy ibn Rabi'ah scoffed: 'Even if I saw the Resurrection with my own eyes, I would never believe that God could gather these rotten bones!', the Qur'an responded by demonstrating that divine omnipotence does not merely reassemble large skeletal limbs, but reconstructs the most delicate, intricate, and unique anatomical details of human biology.
  - Modern forensic science confirms that human fingerprints are biologically unique to every single human being across history; the Creator who designed this unique epidermal signature affirms His absolute power to resurrect every soul in its exact, individual identity.

### Claim: The Protocol of Revelation: La Tuharrik bihi Lisanaka li-Ta'jala Bih (Bukhari 5)
- **Thematic Summary:** How the Prophet's anxious haste to memorize revelation was calmed by divine guarantee.
- **Source Evidence:** Sahih al-Bukhari 5, 4927; Sahih Muslim 448; Tafsir at-Tabari (23/740); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/282).
  - In Sahih al-Bukhari (5, 4927, 5044) on the authority of Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Abdullah ibn Abbas regarding Ayahs 16–19: 'The Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to undergo great hardship when receiving revelation, and he would move his lips rapidly in haste to memorize the words before Gabriel departed.'
  - 'Thereupon Allah revealed: 'Do not move your tongue with it to hasten it. Indeed, upon Us is its collection and its recitation. So when We have recited it, follow its recitation. Then upon Us is its clarification.' (75:16–19).'
  - Ibn Abbas explained: 'Upon Us is its collection' means Allah guaranteed to preserve the entire Qur'an permanently in his heart without losing a single letter; 'So when We recite it, follow its recitation' means listen in reverent silence while Gabriel recites; 'Then upon Us is its clarification' means Allah guaranteed to elucidate its halal, haram, and deeper wisdom through divine inspiration.
  - From that day forward, whenever Gabriel descended, the Prophet ﷺ listened with complete, serene stillness, and when Gabriel departed, the Prophet recited the verses exactly as transmitted without the slightest omission.

### Claim: The Beatific Vision: Wujoohun Yawma'idhin Nadhirah, Ila Rabbiha Nadhirah (Bukhari 7437)
- **Thematic Summary:** The orthodox Sunni consensus on the highest reward of Paradise: beholding the Divine Countenance.
- **Source Evidence:** Sahih al-Bukhari 7437; Sahih Muslim 181; Tafsir at-Tabari (23/745); Sharh al-'Aqeedah at-Tahawiyyah; Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/284).
  - Ayahs 22–23 reveal the supreme joy of the saved: 'Faces on that Day will be radiant [Nadhirah], looking at their Lord [Ila Rabbiha nadhirah].'
  - In Sahih al-Bukhari (7437) and Sahih Muslim (181) from Jarir ibn Abdillah: 'We were sitting with the Prophet ﷺ on a night when the moon was full. He looked up at the moon and said: 'Indeed, you will see your Lord just as you see this moon; you will not struggle or be harmed in seeing Him. So if you are able not to miss a prayer before the rising of the sun (Fajr) and before its setting ('Asr), then do so.' Then he recited: 'And glorify the praise of your Lord before the rising of the sun and before its setting.'
  - Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah hold an immutable consensus based on this text that the believers will genuinely behold Allah with their physical eyes in Paradise without modality (Bi-la kayf), without encompassing Him, and without comparison to created light.
  - The Arabic grammatical syntax 'Nadhara' with 'Ila' uniquely establishes direct visual gazing, contrasting with the disbelievers who are completely veiled from their Lord on that Day (Surah al-Mutaffifin 83:15).

### Claim: The Agony of the Dying Throat: Kalla Idha Balaghati at-Taraqee (75:26–30)
- **Thematic Summary:** The clinical and metaphysical portrait of the deathbed struggle when worldly illusions dissolve.
- **Source Evidence:** Tafsir at-Tabari (23/752); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/288); Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (19/108); Lisan al-'Arab.
  - In Ayahs 26–30, the Qur'an portrays the irreversible threshold of human mortality: 'Nay, when the soul reaches the collarbones [At-Taraqee], and it is said: Who is the healer? [Man raq?], and the dying one concludes that it is the parting [Al-Firaq], and the leg is entwined with the other leg—unto your Lord that Day is the driving march.'
  - Imam al-Tabari explains that 'At-Taraqee' are the clavicle collarbones where the respiratory breath rattles in the final death agony (Sakrat al-Mawt). The frantic family members rush around the bed crying: 'Is there any physician, specialist, or medicine that can cure him?'—yet biological life is terminating.
  - Ibn Abbas explained 'Wat-taffati as-saqu bis-saq' as the entwining of two catastrophic states: the final agony of the departing worldly life conjoined with the first terrifying threshold of the afterlife, or the literal physical entwining and stiffening of the dying person's legs in rigor mortis.
  - The verses strip away human illusions of self-sufficiency: the swaggering oligarch who strutted home to his family in vanity (Yatamatta) is driven like a powerless captive unto the sovereign tribunal of the Lord of Worlds.
