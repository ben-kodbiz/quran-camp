# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 58 (Al-Mujadilah)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Divine Hearing of Khawlah's Plea (58:1–4)
**Thematic Summary:** Qad sami'a Allahu: the cosmic hearing that shattered pagan domestic oppression.

In Sahih al-Bukhari (7386) and Musnad Ahmad (27319), 'A'ishah bint Abi Bakr expressed awe at the cosmic hearing of Allah: 'All praise is for Allah Whose hearing encompasses all voices! The pleading woman (Khawlah bint Tha'labah) came to the Messenger of Allah complaining about her husband Aws ibn as-Samit. I was sitting in the corner of the apartment, and parts of her speech were hidden from me, yet Allah revealed from above seven heavens: "Allah has heard the words of she who disputes with you concerning her husband!"'

This revelation immediately dismantled pagan customs that disempowered vulnerable women, establishing that divine justice hears the whisper of the oppressed above societal tyranny.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 7386 • Sunan Abi Dawud 2214 • Musnad Ahmad 27319 • Asbab an-Nuzul (Wahidi)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Omnipresent Fourth and Sixth in Secret Najwa (58:7)
**Thematic Summary:** Ma yakoonu min najwa thalathatin: unmasking conspiratorial whispers through divine omniscience.

Ayah 7 delivers one of the most sublime descriptions of divine omniscience in the Quran: 'Have you not considered that Allah knows what is in the heavens and what is on the earth? There is in no private conversation three but that He is the fourth of them, nor are there five but that He is the sixth of them, and no less than that and no more but that He is with them wherever they are. Then He will inform them of what they did on the Day of Resurrection.'

Imam at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir explain that this is 'Ma'iyyah bi'l-'Ilm' (Nearness in Knowledge, Observation, and Authority), destroying the illusion that covert conspiracies can evade divine accounting.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir at-Tabari (23/235) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/51) • Al-Asma' was-Sifat

## Exegetical Deep Dive: Sacred Space in Assemblies & The Elevation of Scholars (58:11)
**Thematic Summary:** Tafassahoo fi'l-majalis and the divine elevation of believers gifted with authentic wisdom.

Ayah 11 regulates communal social ethics: 'When you are told to make room in assemblies, make room; Allah will make room for you... Allah will raise those who have believed among you and those who were given knowledge by degrees.'

Classical commentators note this verse was revealed when veterans of the Battle of Badr arrived at the Prophet's crowded assembly and people hesitated to make space. The verse commands generosity of seating, promising reciprocal divine expansion in hearts, graves, and Paradise. Furthermore, it establishes knowledge as the supreme criterion of spiritual elevation.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sunan at-Tirmidhi • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/57) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Wahidi p. 280)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Supreme Charter of Hizbullah (58:22)
**Thematic Summary:** Transcending tribalism through faith written upon the living heart and divine contentment.

Ayah 22 defines the foundational ethos of ideological allegiance: 'You will not find a people who believe in Allah and the Last Day loving those who oppose Allah and His Messenger, even if they were their fathers or their sons or their brothers or their clan.'

Classical biographers note that this was tested at Badr and Uhud, where Abu Ubaydah faced his father who was fighting against Islam, and Abu Bakr faced his son. Allah declared of those whose moral loyalty transcends tribalism: 'Those—He has written upon their hearts faith and supported them with a spirit from Him... Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him. Those are the Party of Allah. Unquestionably, the Party of Allah—they are the successful.'

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari • Sirat Ibn Hisham • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/64) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (17/307)
