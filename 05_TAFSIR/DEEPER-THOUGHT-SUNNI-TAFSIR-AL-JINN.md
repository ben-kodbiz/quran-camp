# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 72 (Al-Jinn)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Dawn Encounter at Nakhlah: Inna Sami'na Qur'anan 'Ajaba (Bukhari 773)
**Thematic Summary:** The historical incident where a company of jinn overheard the Prophet's Fajr recitation after the trauma of Ta'if.

In Sahih al-Bukhari (773) and Sahih Muslim (449) on the authority of Abdullah ibn Abbas: 'The Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not recite to the jinn, nor did he see them. Rather, he set out with a group of his companions heading toward the market of 'Ukaz. At that time, something intervened between the devils and the news of heaven, and flaming meteors were sent against them.'

'The devils returned to their people, who said: What is the matter with you? They replied: Something has intervened between us and the news of heaven, and meteors were hurled at us! Their elders said: Nothing has intervened between you and the news of heaven except some monumental event that has occurred in the earth. Traverse the earth from East to West and see what has happened!'

'The group that headed toward Tihamah came upon the Prophet ﷺ while he was at a place called Nakhlah, on his return from Ta'if, leading his companions in the Dawn Prayer (Fajr). When they heard the Qur'an, they listened intently to it and exclaimed: By Allah, this is what intervened between you and the news of heaven!'

'They returned to their people and declared: O our people! Indeed, we have heard a wondrous Qur'an! It guides to right conduct, so we have believed in it, and we will never associate anyone with our Lord!' Thereupon Allah revealed to His Prophet: 'Qul oohiya ilayya annahu-stama'a nafarun minal-jinn.' (72:1-2).

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 773; Sahih Muslim 449; Tafsir at-Tabari (23/650); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/242).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Celestial Airspace Sealed: Harasan Shadeedan wa Shuhuba (Bukhari 4921)
**Thematic Summary:** How celestial shooting stars were weaponized to terminate occult eavesdropping upon the descent of the Qur'an.

In Ayahs 8–9, the jinn recount their cosmic shock: 'And we sought to reach the heaven, but found it filled with strict guards and flaming meteors. And we used to sit there in positions for hearing, but whoever listens now will find a flaming meteor lying in wait for him.'

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4921) on the authority of Abu Hurairah, the Prophet ﷺ explained how devils used to eavesdrop before Islam: they stood upon one another's shoulders in a vertical human-jinn ladder reaching near the lowest heaven. When Allah decreed a matter in heaven, the angels struck their wings in awe, and the word descended through the heavens until an eavesdropping devil caught a single word and whispered it to the one below him, until it reached the soothsayer on earth.

Often a flaming meteor struck and incinerated the demon before he could whisper it; but sometimes he passed the word before being struck, and the soothsayer added to that single word one hundred lies.

When the mission of Muhammad ﷺ commenced, divine decree stationed colossal archangelic guards ('Harasan Shadeedan') and intensified meteoric barrages ('Shuhuban Rasada') so that the celestial channels were hermetically sealed, ensuring zero demonic contamination of divine revelation.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 4921; Tafsir al-Tabari (23/656); Fath al-Bari (8/672); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/245).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Universal Charter of Monotheism: Wa Annal-Masajida Lillahi (72:18)
**Thematic Summary:** The strict theological prohibition against invoking anyone or anything alongside Allah in places of worship.

Ayah 18 proclaims: 'And indeed, the places of worship [Al-Masajid] are for Allah, so do not invoke with Allah anyone [Fala tad'oo ma'allahi ahada].'

Exegetes provide three complementary interpretations of 'Al-Masajid': (1) The physical buildings constructed for prayer, specifically mosques; (2) The bodily limbs upon which human beings prostrate (forehead, nose, hands, knees, and toes), as recorded by Sa'id ibn Jubayr; (3) The entire terrestrial globe, referencing the Prophetic tradition: 'The earth has been made for me a masjid and a purifier.'

The negative particle 'La' combined with the indefinite pronoun 'Ahada' in the context of prohibition constitutes an absolute grammatical universal (Sighat al-'Umum): it prohibits the invocation, supplication, petition, or deification of any created entity whatsoever—whether an archangel, a messenger, a saint, a jinn, or an ancestral king.

Imam al-Qurtubi emphasizes that this verse is the architectural and liturgical constitution of every Islamic house of worship: mosques are sanctuaries of pure monotheism, and any practice that introduces prayers to the deceased or occult forces invalidates their sacred purpose.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/318); Tafsir al-Tabari (23/662); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/248); Ahkam al-Qur'an lil-Jassas.

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Fortress of Revelation: 'Alimu al-Ghaybi & The Angelic Guards (72:26–28)
**Thematic Summary:** How Allah reserves knowledge of the unseen and dispatches guardian angels to protect chosen messengers.

Ayahs 26–28 present the sovereign epistemology of prophecy: '[He is] Knower of the unseen, and He does not disclose His unseen to anyone, except whom He has approved of messengers, and indeed, He sends before each messenger and behind him observers [Rasada], that he may know that they have conveyed the messages of their Lord.'

Imam Fakhr al-Din al-Razi in Mafatih al-Ghayb and Ibn Kathir explain that 'Al-Ghayb' (the absolute unseen—such as the exact hour of Resurrection, the decrees of destiny, and the unseen realms) is the exclusive domain of Allah. When Allah chooses to reveal metaphysical truths (such as Paradise, Hell, and angelic realities), He discloses them exclusively to approved Messengers via revelation.

To prevent demons or jinn from intercepting, imitating, or whispering into this sacred transmission, Allah surrounds the Prophet ﷺ with an impenetrable phalanx of angels ('Rasada') guarding his front, back, and celestial airspace.

The Surah concludes with the sublime doxology of divine omniscience: 'Wa ahata bi-ma ladayhim wa ahsa kulla shay'in 'adada'—Allah's knowledge encompasses everything they possess and He has recorded every single particle and atom in the cosmos with absolute numerical precision.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/252); Mafatih al-Ghayb (Al-Razi 30/172); Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/325); Kitab at-Tawhid.
