# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 59 (Al-Hashr)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Expulsion of Banu an-Nadir (59:2–5)
**Thematic Summary:** Surat an-Nadir: when fortified strongholds crumbled without sword or spear.

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4882), Sa'id ibn Jubayr asked Abdullah ibn Abbas about Surat al-Hashr. Ibn Abbas replied emphatically: 'Say Surat an-Nadir!' It was revealed specifically regarding the siege and expulsion of the Jewish tribe Banu an-Nadir following their conspiracy to drop a boulder on the Prophet's head in Medina.

Despite possessing colossal stone fortresses and towering date orchards, divine awe paralyzed their resolve within days. They negotiated their own exile, demolishing the lintels and beams of their own mansions to salvage wood, fulfilling the prophecy: 'They destroy their houses with their own hands.'

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 4882 • Sahih Muslim 1757 • Sirat Ibn Hisham • Asbab an-Nuzul (Wahidi p. 282)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Economic Charter of Fai' & De-Monopolization (59:7)
**Thematic Summary:** Kay la yakoona doolatan bayna al-aghniya': preventing the concentration of capital.

Ayah 7 enacts one of the most foundational socio-economic principles in revealed law. War booty acquired without direct cavalry engagement (Fai') was not divided among frontline soldiers, but dedicated to the collective treasury for the poor, orphans, and wayfarers.

The divine rationale is immortalized: 'So that it does not become a commodity circulating merely between the rich among you.' Classical commentators like Imam al-Qurtubi emphasize that this verse establishes the state's ethical responsibility to obstruct oligarchic wealth accumulation and redistribute capital to elevate vulnerable strata.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/14) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/72) • Ahkam al-Qur'an (Jassas)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Monumental Altruism of the Ansar (59:9)
**Thematic Summary:** Yu'thiroona 'ala anfusihim: the golden zenith of self-sacrificial love.

Ayah 9 immortalizes the Medinan Helpers (Al-Ansar) who welcomed the destitute Meccan refugees with unmatched generosity. In Sahih al-Bukhari (3798) and Sahih Muslim (2054), an impoverished guest came to the Prophet, who had nothing in his household. An Ansari took the guest home, instructed his wife to put the children to sleep, dimmed the oil lamp, and pretended to eat alongside the guest so the guest could eat to his fill.

The following morning, the Prophet smiled and said: 'Allah laughed in joy and approved of your conduct last night!' The verse was revealed: 'And they prefer them over themselves, even though they are in dire poverty.'

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 3798 • Sahih Muslim 2054 • Tafsir at-Tabari (23/270)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Submissive Mountain & The Most Beautiful Names (59:21–24)
**Thematic Summary:** Law anzalna hadha al-Qur'ana 'ala jabal: the shattering weight of divine reality.

Ayah 21 presents a staggering metaphor: if the living Quran were cast onto a solid granite mountain, the colossal rock would crack, crumble, and dissolve into dust out of absolute reverence (Khashyah) for Allah. How strange then is the human heart, fashioned of soft tissue, that listens to divine speech without quivering!

The Surah concludes with the magnificent litany of sixteen divine names, crowning monotheism: Al-Malik, Al-Quddoos, As-Salam, Al-Mu'min, Al-Muhaymin, Al-'Azeez, Al-Jabbar, Al-Mutakabbir, Al-Khaliq, Al-Bari', Al-Musawwir. In Jami' at-Tirmidhi (2922), whoever recites these concluding verses in morning or evening has seventy thousand angels supplicating for their mercy.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2922 • Musnad Ahmad • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/82) • Mafatih al-Ghayb
