# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 66 (At-Tahrim)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Domestic Crisis of Prohibition & The Honey Incident (66:1–4)
**Thematic Summary:** Lima tuharrimu ma ahalla Allah: the divine defense of lawful marital peace.

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4912) and Sahih Muslim (1474), 'A'ishah narrated that the Prophet ﷺ used to spend time with his wife Zaynab bint Jahsh and drink honey at her apartment. 'A'ishah and Hafsah agreed together that whichever of them the Prophet visited first, she would say: 'I smell the odor of Maghafeer (a pungent resin) on you; have you eaten Maghafeer?' When the Prophet heard this from both, he said: 'No, but I drank honey at Zaynab's house, and I swear an oath that I will never drink it again!' He asked them not to mention this to anyone.

Yet Hafsah disclosed the matter to 'A'ishah. Allah revealed the opening of Surah At-Tahrim, gently reproaching the Prophet for forbidding what God made lawful, ordering him to expiate his oath, and calling both wives to sincere repentance for their domestic intrigue.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 4912 • Sahih Muslim 1474 • Sunan an-Nasa'i 3959 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/160)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Monumental Celestial Coalition (66:4)
**Thematic Summary:** In tadhahara 'alayhi: when the Archangel and armies mobilize for the Prophet.

Ayah 4 warns the two wives against domestic collusion: 'If you two cooperate against him—then indeed Allah is his protector, and Gabriel and the righteous among the believers and the angels, moreover, are his assistants.'

Umar ibn al-Khattab narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari (4913) that an Ansari neighbor rushed to his door at night crying: 'A terrible thing has happened!' Umar asked in terror: 'Have the Ghassanids arrived to attack us?' The Ansari said: 'No, worse than that: the Prophet has divorced his wives!' Umar rushed to Medina, confronted his daughter Hafsah who was weeping, and rebuked her. The verse reveals that the emotional and spiritual well-being of the Prophet is a matter of cosmic priority defended by the Lord of the Throne Himself.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 4913 • Sahih Muslim 1479 • Tafsir at-Tabari (23/485) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Wahidi p. 290)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Four Archetypal Women of History (66:10–12)
**Thematic Summary:** Two women in holy homes who perished, and two women in tyrannies who attained Eden.

Surah At-Tahrim concludes with four profound female archetypes that shatter tribal and patriarchal determinism: (1) The wife of Noah (Wahilah) and (2) The wife of Lot (Wa'ilah)—both married to towering prophets, yet their biological proximity availed them nothing before God because they betrayed the monotheistic mission. This served as a chilling warning to the Prophet's wives that holy matrimony is no guarantee of salvation without personal righteousness.

Conversely, Allah presents: (3) Asiya, the wife of Pharaoh, who lived in the palace of the greatest tyrant on earth, yet prayed under torture: 'My Lord, build for me near You a house in Paradise and save me from Pharaoh!'—and (4) Maryam bint 'Imran, who lived as an ascetic orphan, preserved her chastity, and believed in the words of her Lord. Salvation is strictly an individual moral responsibility.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 3411 • Sahih Muslim 2431 • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/172) • Al-Bahr al-Muheet

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Universal Sanctuary of Tawbah Nasoohah (66:8)
**Thematic Summary:** Tooboo ila Allahi tawbatan nasooha: the spiritual alchemy of pure return.

Ayah 8 commands: 'O you who have believed, repent to Allah with sincere repentance (Tawbatan Nasoohan). Perhaps your Lord will remove from you your misdeeds and admit you into gardens beneath which rivers flow.' When asked about Nasooh repentance, Umar ibn al-Khattab and Ibn Mas'ud explained: 'It is that a person repents of a bad deed with deep remorse, resolves never to return to it, and does not return to it—just as extracted milk never returns to the teat.'

The verse paints the radiant scene upon the Sirat where believers, seeing the hypocrites' lights extinguished, pray with trembling hope: 'Our Lord, perfect for us our light and forgive us! Indeed, You are over all things Omnipotent.' Sincere repentance in this life ensures unbroken illumination in the Next.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Musnad Ahmad • Sunan Ibn Majah • Tafsir at-Tabari (23/495) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/197)
