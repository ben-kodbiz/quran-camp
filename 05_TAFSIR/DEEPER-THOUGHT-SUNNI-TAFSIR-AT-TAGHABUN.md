# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 64 (At-Taghabun)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Day of Mutual Disillusion: At-Taghabun (64:9)
**Thematic Summary:** Dhalika yawmu at-taghabun: the ultimate cosmic marketplace of winners and losers.

Ayah 9 names the Day of Resurrection 'Yawm at-Taghabun' (The Day of Mutual Loss and Gain, or Mutual Disillusion). The Arabic root Ghabn refers to commercial transactions where one party is cheated or realizes they made a catastrophic bargain. On that Day, the veil is torn away: disbelievers who traded their souls for worldly wealth, titles, and comfort realize they made the worst investment in history.

Imam at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir explain that even the righteous will experience a form of Ghabn, grieving: 'Why did I not sacrifice even more when the returns are so astronomical?' It is the day when human beings see the true exchange rate between transient Dunya and eternal Akhirah.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir at-Tabari (23/415) • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/140) • Al-Mufradat fee Gharib al-Qur'an (Raghib)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: Serenity in the Crucible of Calamity (64:11)
**Thematic Summary:** Ma asaba min museebatin illa bi'idhni Allah: the spiritual science of the guided heart.

Ayah 11 provides the ultimate solace for suffering: 'No calamity strikes except by the permission of Allah. And whoever believes in Allah—He guides his heart. And Allah is Knowing of all things.' When tragedy strikes—loss of wealth, death of loved ones, or debilitating disease—human beings naturally experience shock and anguish.

The great Tabi'i Alqamah ibn Qays explained the guided heart: 'It is the person who, when struck by a catastrophe, realizes deep within his soul: This is from my Lord!—and so he submits with contentment and peace.' Faith does not eliminate pain, but it banishes nihilism, reassuring the believer that suffering is purposeful and governed by divine wisdom.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/142) • Hilyat al-Awliya (Alqamah) • Madarij as-Salikeen

## Exegetical Deep Dive: Domestic Affection as a Spiritual Hazard (64:14–15)
**Thematic Summary:** Inna min azwajikum wa awladikum 'aduwwan lakum: when love becomes an obstacle to truth.

In Jami' at-Tirmidhi (3317), Abdullah ibn Abbas explained the historical background of Ayah 14: 'There were men in Mecca who embraced Islam and wanted to migrate to Medina to join the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. But their wives and children wept, pleaded, and said: "We beseech you by God not to leave us with no one to care for us!" So the men stayed behind out of emotional attachment. Later, when they finally migrated and saw how far the early companions had advanced in religious knowledge and spiritual rank, they wanted to punish their families.'

Thereupon Allah revealed: 'O you who have believed, indeed, among your spouses and your children are enemies to you, so beware of them. But if you pardon and overlook and forgive—then indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.' The verse teaches that emotional love becomes an 'enemy' when it coerces a believer to disobey God, yet commands magnanimous forgiveness rather than domestic bitterness.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3317 • Sunan an-Nasa'i • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/144) • Asbab an-Nuzul (Wahidi p. 287)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Realistic Metric of Taqwa & The Goodly Loan (64:16–17)
**Thematic Summary:** Fattaqoo Allaha ma istata'tum: divine realism and the multiplication of sincere charity.

Ayah 16 delivers one of the most comforting and liberating verses in the Quran: 'So fear Allah as much as you are able, and listen and obey and spend; it is better for your own souls.' Classical scholars note that when Ayah 102 of Surah Ali 'Imran descended ('Fear Allah as He deserves to be feared'), the companions were overwhelmed, weeping and saying: 'Who among us can fulfill this?' Allah revealed this verse in At-Taghabun to establish realistic human capacity while demanding authentic effort.

The Surah concludes with the metaphor of the Goodly Loan (Qard Hasan): 'If you loan Allah a goodly loan, He will multiply it for you and forgive you. And Allah is Most Appreciative (Shakur) and Forbearing (Haleem).' Allah borrows from that which He Himself provided, and rewards the lender with multiplied gardens and total pardon.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/146) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/143) • Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an
