# Classical Sunni Exegetical Monograph: Surah Al-Qasas
### The Subversion of Tyranny: The Nile Basket, Chivalry in Madyan & The Collapse of Qarun

---

## 1. The Opening Letters, Divine Subversion & The Oppressed Vanguard

Surah Al-Qasas opens with the mysterious disjointed letters *Ta-Seen-Meem*, uniting this chapter with *Ash-Shu'ara* and *An-Naml* in the sacred triad of the *Tawaseem*. Allah immediately declares:
*Tilka ayatu al-Kitabi al-mubeen. Natloo 'alayka min naba'i Musa wa Fir'awna bil-haqqi li-qawmin yu'minoon*
("These are the verses of the clear Book. We recite to you from the news of Musa and Pharaoh in truth for a people who believe").

The sociological landscape of Egypt under Pharaoh is diagnosed with forensic precision:
*Inna Fir'awna 'ala fil-ardi wa ja'ala ahlaha shiya'an yastad'ifu ta'ifatan minhum yudhabbihu abna'ahum wa yastahyi nisa'ahum, innahu kana mina al-mufsideen*
("Indeed, Pharaoh exalted himself in the land and made its people into divided factions, oppressing a sector among them, slaughtering their sons and keeping their women alive. Indeed, he was of the corruptors").

Imam al-Tabari and Imam Ibn Kathir explain that Pharaoh established an institutional caste hierarchy, dividing society into competing sects (*Shiya'an*) to prevent unified resistance. Fearing an ancient prophecy that an Israelite youth would dismantle his empire, he instituted systemic state infanticide.

Against this suffocating totalitarian machine, Allah declares His sovereign, immutable decree:
*Wa nureedu an namunna 'ala alladhina-stud'ifu fil-ardi wa naj'alahum a'immatan wa naj'alahum al-waritheen, wa numakkina lahum fil-ardi wa nuriya Fir'awna wa Hamana wa junudahuma minhum ma kanoo yahdharoon*
("And We wished to confer favor upon those who were oppressed in the land and make them leaders and make them inheritors, and establish them in the land and show Pharaoh and Haman and their soldiers through them that which they feared").

This verse forms the master theological pillar of the surah: worldly power, military dominance, and economic monopolies are fragile illusions before the divine will. Allah elevates the marginalized (*Al-Mustad'afeen*), transforming the hunted into ideological guides and heirs of the earth.

## 2. The River of Trust, Asiyah's Sanctuary & The Emptied Heart

The manifestation of divine cunning unfolds not through celestial armies, but through an infant in a fragile wooden chest floating on the Nile:
*Wa awhayna ila ummi Musa an ardi'eehi fa-idha khifti 'alayhi fa-alqihi fil-yammi wa la takhafi wa la tahzani, inna raaddoohu ilayki wa ja'iloohu mina al-mursaleen*
("And We inspired the mother of Musa: 'Suckle him; and when you fear for him, cast him into the river and do not fear and do not grieve; indeed, We will return him to you and will make him one of the messengers'").

Imam al-Qurtubi explains that this inspiration was *Ilham* (divine intuitive casting into the heart), akin to the inspiration given to the bee. The command contains an astonishing pairing: two commands (*Suckle him, Cast him*), two prohibitions (*Do not fear, Do not grieve*), and two divine promises (*We will return him, We will make him a messenger*).

The wooden basket drifts directly into the private water channel of Pharaoh's palace:
*Fa-iltaqatahu alu Fir'awna li-yakuna lahum 'aduwwan wa hazana*
("Then the family of Pharaoh picked him up so that he might become to them an enemy and a cause of grief").
This is *Lam al-'Aqibah* (the 'L' of consequence): Pharaoh believed he was adopting a defenseless infant for pleasure, but in divine providence, he was personally funding, feeding, and sheltering the architect of his own destruction.

When Pharaoh intended execution, his righteous wife Asiyah intervened:
*Qurratu 'aynin lee wa lak, la taqtuloohu 'asa an yanfa'ana aw nattakhidhahu walada, wa hum la yash'uroon*
("A comfort of the eye for me and for you! Do not kill him; perhaps he will benefit us, or we will adopt him as a son. And they perceived not").
The Prophet ﷺ praised Asiyah as one of the four perfected women of creation (Sahih al-Bukhari). Her maternal intercession became the divine armor preserving Musa within the tyrant's lair.

Meanwhile, Musa's biological mother experienced acute psychological trauma:
*Wa asbaha fu'adu ummi Musa farighan in kadat la-tubdee bihi lawla an rabatna 'ala qalbiha li-takuna mina al-mu'mineen*
("And the heart of Musa's mother became empty. She had nearly disclosed his identity had We not bound her heart that she would be of the believers").
Imam al-Tabari notes that *Farighan* means her heart was evacuated of all thoughts, anxieties, and worldly awareness except the thought of Musa. She was on the verge of crying out in despair, but Allah tied firm bonds upon her heart (*Rabatna 'ala qalbiha*)—granting divine serenity (*Sakinah*) to preserve the divine plan.

## 3. The Sister's Stratagem & The Restoration of the Divine Promise

To track the infant's fate, the mother commanded Musa's sister: *Qusseeh* ("Follow him"). The young girl executed this mission with extraordinary intelligence:
*Fa-basurat bihi 'an junubin wa hum la yash'uroon*
("So she watched him from a distance while they did not perceive").
She did not follow the chest directly, which would arouse suspicion from imperial patrols, but observed from an oblique angle (*'An junub*), pretending to be an ordinary passerby.

Within the palace, the infant cried out in hunger, yet:
*Wa harramna 'alayhi al-maradi'a min qablu*
("And We had prevented from him all wet nurses before").
Allah placed an instinctual aversion in the infant's palate against any breast other than his mother's. Imperial physicians and royal wet-nurses were brought from across Egypt, but the child rejected them all.

Seeing their desperation, the sister stepped forward with sublime tactical adab:
*Hal adullukum 'ala ahli baytin yakfuloonahu lakum wa hum lahu nasihoon?*
("Shall I direct you to a household that will rear him for you and be sincere to him?").
She did not say "his mother," but referred to a noble family that would serve the royal court faithfully.

Musa was brought to his mother's bosom, and he latched immediately:
*Fa-radadnahu ila ummihi kay taqarra 'aynuha wa la tahzan wa li-ta'lama anna wa'da Allahi haqqun wa lakinna aktharahum la ya'lamoon*
("So We restored him to his mother that she might be comforted and not grieve, and that she would know that the promise of Allah is true, but most of them do not know").
Musa returned to his mother's arms, suckling at state expense, protected by the royal decree, validating that Allah's promises are inexorable reality.

## 4. The Egyptian Crisis, The Accidental Blow & The Conscience of Repentance

Musa grew into a prince of Egypt:
*Wa lamma balagha ashuddahu was-tawa ataynahu hukman wa 'ilma, wa kadhalika najzi al-muhsineen*
("And when he reached full maturity and was firmly established, We gave him judgment and knowledge. And thus do We reward the doers of good").
Imam al-Baghawi notes that *Ashudd* represents the zenith of physical vitality (around age 30–33), and *Istawa* represents intellectual and moral completion (around age 40).

One afternoon during the quiet heat when the city was at rest, Musa walked through the streets and encountered two men fighting: one from his own community (Banu Isra'il) and one from his enemies (an Egyptian Coptic overseer). The Israelite called upon Musa for assistance:
*Fa-wakazahu Musa fa-qada 'alayh*
("So Musa struck him with a clenched fist and caused his death").

Musa did not intend murder; his extraordinary physical strength turned an intervention into an accidental fatality. Notice his instantaneous spiritual response:
*Qala hadha min 'amali ash-shaytani, innahu 'aduwwun mudillun mubeen. Qala rabbi inni zalamtu nafsi faghfir lee, fa-ghafara lah, innahu huwa al-ghafooru ar-raheem*
("He said: 'This is from the work of Satan; indeed, he is a clear, misleading enemy.' He said: 'My Lord, indeed I have wronged myself, so forgive me,' and He forgave him. Indeed, He is the Forgiving, the Merciful").

Musa did not rationalize the killing, invoke tribal solidarity, or celebrate the demise of an oppressor. He instantly recognized the impulse of anger as satanic, took full moral ownership, and wept in repentance. Allah accepted his tawbah immediately.

Musa then took a solemn lifelong covenant of social ethics:
*Qala rabbi bima an'amta 'alayya fa-lan akuna zaheeran lil-mujrimeen*
("He said: 'My Lord, for the favor You have bestowed upon me, I will never be an ally to the criminals'").
Imam Ibn al-Qayyim deduces from this verse that a believer must never lend their physical strength, professional expertise, political influence, or legal skill to bolster tyrannical, unjust, or corrupt institutions.

## 5. The Sinai Flight, The Well of Madyan & The Du'a of Absolute Need

The following morning, fear gripped the city:
*Fa-asbaha fil-madeenati kha'ifan yataraqqabu*
("So he became in the city fearful and watchful").
The same Israelite was involved in another brawl and called again. Musa rebuked him: *Innaka la-ghawiyyun mubeen* ("Indeed, you are a clear instigator!"). When Musa moved to restrain the Egyptian, the man panicked, thinking Musa intended to kill him, and blurted out: *"Do you intend to kill me as you killed a man yesterday? You only want to be a tyrant in the land!"*

The secret was exposed. Pharaoh's ruling council met to sign an executive warrant of execution. At this critical juncture, Allah sent an unsung hero:
*Wa ja'a rajulun min aqsa al-madeenati yas'a qala ya Musa inna al-mala'a ya'tamiruna bika li-yaqtulooka fakhruj inni laka mina an-nasiheen*
("And there came a man running from the farthest part of the city. He said: 'O Musa, indeed the ruling council is conspiring to kill you, so flee; indeed, I am to you of the sincere advisers'").

Musa fled immediately without provisions, camel, or navigation across the desert of Sinai, crying out:
*Rabbi najjini mina al-qawmi az-zalimeen* ("My Lord, save me from the wrongdoing people").
Heading toward Madyan, he prayed: *'Asa rabbi an yahdiyani sawa'a as-sabeel* ("Perhaps my Lord will guide me to the right course").

Reaching the desert oasis of Madyan, Musa observed a crowd of male shepherds jostling aggressively around the main well. Behind them stood two young women holding back their flock (*Tadhudaan*):
*Qala ma khatbukuma? Qalata la nasqee hatta yusdira ar-ri'a'u wa aboona shaykhun kabeer*
("He said: 'What is your situation?' They said: 'We cannot water our flocks until the shepherds depart, and our father is an elderly old man'").

Notice their dignity: they did not complain of poverty, but explained that physical vulnerability and modesty prevented them from mingling with aggressive male herdsmen.

Musa did not hesitate:
*Fa-saqa lahuma thumma tawalla ila az-zill*
("So he drew water for them, then withdrew to the shade").
Despite profound dehydration, blistered feet, and exhaustion, Musa lifted the heavy stone covering the well and watered their entire flock. He demanded no fee, no thank you, and initiated no flirtatious banter. He immediately retreated into the shade of an acacia tree.

There, exhausted and destitute, Musa uttered the masterpiece du'a of existential humility:
*Rabbi inni lima anzalta ilayya min khayrin faqeer*
("My Lord, indeed I am in desperate need of whatever good You send down upon me").
Imam Ibn Kathir and Imam al-Qurtubi emphasize that Musa was so hungry his stomach touched his spine, yet he did not specify bread or gold; he declared himself a beggar (*Faqeer*) before whatever divine grace Allah might bestow.

## 6. Sacred Modesty, The Hiring Standard & The Ten-Year Covenant

Musa's du'a was answered almost instantaneously:
*Fa-ja'at-hu ihdahuma tamshi 'ala istihya'in qalat inna abee yad'ooka li-yajziyaka ajra ma saqayta lana*
("Then one of the two women came to him walking with modesty. She said: 'Indeed, my father calls you that he may reward you with the payment for having watered for us'").

Imam 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (may Allah be pleased with him) remarked upon this verse: *"She was not bold, brash, or exhibitionist; she came covering her face with the sleeve of her garment, walking with innate modesty"* (Tafsir Ibn Kathir).

Musa accompanied her to their venerable father (identified in classical exegesis as Shu'ayb or a righteous elder of Madyan). Musa recounted his story:
*Qala la takhaf, najawta mina al-qawmi az-zalimeen* ("Do not fear; you have escaped from the wrongdoing people").

At this juncture, the perceptive daughter delivered the universal formula of human resource management and institutional governance:
*Ya abati-sta'jirhu, inna khayra mani-sta'jarta al-qawiyyu al-ameen*
("O my father, hire him! Indeed, the best of those you can hire is the strong and the trustworthy").

Imam Ibn Taymiyyah in *Al-Siyasah al-Shar'iyyah* and Imam Ibn al-Qayyim explain that this verse encapsulates the twin pillars of every public office, business partnership, and institutional leadership:
1. **Al-Quwwah (Competence / Capability):** Physical strength, technical knowledge, strategic foresight, and execution skill appropriate to the task.
2. **Al-Amanah (Trustworthiness / Integrity):** God-fearing honesty, moral uprightness, incorruptibility, and loyalty.
When either pillar collapses, organizations and nations decay.

The father offered Musa one of his daughters in marriage on the condition of an eight-to-ten year service covenant:
*Qala inni ureedu an unkihaka ihda ibnatayya hatayni 'ala an ta'jurani thamaniya hijaj, fa-in atmamta 'ashran fa-min 'indik*
("He said: 'Indeed, I wish to marry you to one of these two daughters of mine, on the condition that you serve me for eight years; but if you complete ten, it will be from your own generosity'").
Musa agreed, and Sahih al-Bukhari records that Musa fulfilled the ten complete years—always opting for the higher, most generous standard of prophetic honor.

## 7. The Fire at Mount Tur, Pharaoh's Tower & The Law of Guidance

Having completed the term, Musa set forth with his family across the Sinai peninsula. On a freezing night, he saw a fire on the slopes of Mount Tur:
*Fa-lamma qada Musa al-ajala wa sara bi-ahlihi anasa min janibi at-turi nara*
("And when Musa had completed the term and was traveling with his family, he saw in the direction of the Mount a fire").

Approaching the flame, he was addressed from the right bank of the blessed valley from a bush:
*Falamma ataha noodiya min shati'i al-wadi al-aymani fil-buq'ati al-mubarakati mina ash-shajarati an ya Musa inni ana Allahu rabbu al-'alameen*
("And when he came to it, he was called from the right side of the valley in a blessed spot from the tree: 'O Musa, indeed I am Allah, Lord of the worlds'").

Musa was granted the miraculous signs of the staff turning into a serpent and his hand emerging luminously white. Commanded to confront Pharaoh, Musa petitioned for his eloquent brother Harun: *Fa-arsilhu ma'iya rid'an yusaddiqunee* ("So send him with me as a helper to confirm me").

Standing before Pharaoh, the tyrant met the divine truth with supreme mockery:
*Wa qala Fir'awnu ya ayyuha al-mala'u ma 'alimtu lakum min ilahin ghayri, fa-awqid lee ya Hamanu 'ala at-teeni faj'al lee sarhan la'alli attali'u ila ilahi Musa wa inni la-azunnuhu mina al-kadhibeen*
("And Pharaoh said: 'O nobles, I have not known for you any god other than me! So ignite for me, O Haman, [a fire upon] the clay and make for me a tower that I may look up to the God of Musa. And indeed, I think he is of the liars'").

Pharaoh demanded baked bricks to build a ziggurat tower (*Sarh*) to gaze at the heavens—an archetypal monument to totalitarian narcissism. But his arrogance ended in swift retribution:
*Fa-akhadhnahu wa junudahu fa-nabadhnahum fil-yammi fanzur kayfa kana 'aqibatu az-zalimeen*
("So We seized him and his soldiers and threw them into the sea. So see how was the end of the wrongdoers").
Allah declared them *A'immatan yad'oona ila an-nar*—leaders who invite to the Fire, stripped of assistance on the Day of Resurrection.

Later in the surah, Allah comforts the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ when his beloved uncle Abu Talib died refusing the Shahadah:
*Innaka la tahdi man ahbabta wa lakinna Allaha yahdi man yasha'u wa huwa a'lamu bil-muhtadeen*
("Indeed, you cannot guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills, and He is most knowing of the guided").
This verse establishes the supreme theological reality that human effort cannot conquer spiritual recalcitrance; guidance (*Hidayah*) is a sovereign gift bestowed by Allah upon receptive souls.

## 8. The Hubris of Qarun, The Sinking Earth & The Eternal Standard

The climax of the surah shifts from political totalitarianism (Pharaoh) to capitalistic arrogance in the figure of Qarun:
*Inna Qaruna kana min qawmi Musa fabagha 'alayhim, wa ataynahu mina al-kunoori ma inna mafatihahu la-tanoo'u bil-'usbati uli al-quwwah*
("Indeed, Qarun was from the people of Musa, but he tyrannized them. And We gave him of treasures whose very keys would burden a company of strong men").

The sincere scholars among Banu Isra'il offered Qarun a five-point ethical framework for wealth management:
1. *La tafrah inna Allaha la yuhibbu al-fariheen* (Do not exult arrogantly; Allah does not love the boastful).
2. *Wabtaghi feema ataka Allahu ad-dara al-akhirah* (Direct your resources toward the eternal Home of the Hereafter).
3. *Wa la tansa naseebaka mina ad-dunya* (Do not neglect your lawful, healthy share of worldly life).
4. *Wa ahsin kama ahsana Allahu ilayk* (Do good to others as Allah has been generous to you).
5. *Wa la tabghi al-fasada fil-ard* (Do not sow corruption or exploitation in the land).

Qarun rejected this spiritual wisdom with the iconic slogan of predatory capitalism:
*Qala innama ooteetuhu 'ala 'ilmin 'indi*
("He said: 'I was only given this because of knowledge that I possess!'").
He attributed his immense wealth entirely to his own intellect, shrewdness, and personal merit, dismissing divine grace.

Parading in pomp with his retinue, the worldly onlookers gasped: *Ya layta lana mithla ma ootiya Qarun* ("Oh, would that we had the like of what Qarun has been given!"). But the scholars responded: *Waylakum thawabullahi khayrun liman amana wa 'amila saliha* ("Woe to you! The reward of Allah is better for he who believes and does righteousness").

Then came the sudden, catastrophic judgment:
*Fa-khasafna bihi wa bi-darihi al-ard, fama kana lahu min fi'atin yansuroonahu min doonillahi wa ma kana mina al-muntasireen*
("So We caused the earth to swallow him and his home. And there was for him no company to aid him against Allah, nor was he of those who could defend themselves").
The earth liquefied beneath him. The onlookers who had envied him only yesterday shuddered in realization: *Wayka'anna Allaha yabsutu ar-rizqa liman yasha'u min 'ibadihi wa yaqdir* ("Ah, it is indeed Allah who expands provision for whom He wills and restricts it!").

The surah culminates in the eternal constitutional charter of salvation:
*Tilka ad-daru al-akhiratu naj'aluha lilladhina la yureedoona 'uluwwan fil-ardi wa la fasada, wal-'aqibatu lil-muttaqeen*
("That Home of the Hereafter We assign to those who desire not high-handed arrogance upon the earth nor corruption. And the ultimate outcome is for the righteous").

As the Prophet ﷺ faced expulsion from Mecca, Allah granted him the sovereign comfort:
*Inna alladhi farada 'alayka al-Qur'ana la-raadduka ila ma'ad*
("Indeed, He who ordained the Qur'an upon you will surely return you to an ultimate destination").
The chapter closes with the ultimate doxological reality:
*Kullu shay'in halikun illa wajhah, lahu al-hukmu wa ilayhi turja'oon*
("Everything will perish except His Face. His is the judgment, and to Him you will be returned").
