# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 71 (Nuh)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Genealogy of Idolatry: Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq & Nasr (Bukhari 4920)
**Thematic Summary:** How the devil weaponized love for righteous scholars to invent polytheism on earth.

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4920), Abdullah ibn Abbas provided the foundational canonical exegesis of Ayah 23: 'And they said: Never abandon your gods, and never abandon Wadd, nor Suwa', nor Yaghuth, nor Ya'uq, nor Nasr.'

Ibn Abbas testified: 'These were names of righteous men from the people of Noah. When they died, Satan whispered to their people: Erect statues in the places where they used to sit, and name those statues after them. So they did so, but the statues were not yet worshiped.'

'However, when that generation passed away and sacred knowledge was forgotten, the statues began to be worshiped as divine intermediaries.' Ibn Abbas noted that these very idols were later resurrected among pagan Arab tribes during Jahiliyyah: Wadd belonged to Kalb, Suwa' to Hudhayl, Yaghuth to Murad, Ya'uq to Hamdan, and Nasr to Himyar.

This tradition serves as a foundational axiom of Islamic monotheism: polytheism does not begin with the conscious desire to worship stones, but with the subtle, emotional exaggeration of righteous saints until the boundary of divine transcendence is dissolved.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 4920; Tafsir al-Tabari (23/635); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/234); Fath al-Bari (8/668).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: Hydrological & Economic Deliverance: The Power of Istighfar (71:10–12)
**Thematic Summary:** How seeking divine forgiveness unlocks atmospheric precipitation, financial ease, and progeny.

In Ayahs 10–12, Noah proclaims to his people: 'Ask forgiveness of your Lord, indeed He is Ever-Forgiving. He will send [rain from] the sky upon you in showers, and give you increase in wealth and children and provide for you gardens and provide for you rivers.'

Imam al-Qurtubi in Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an records that a man came to Al-Hasan al-Basri complaining of drought, and Al-Hasan said: 'Seek forgiveness of Allah.' Another came complaining of poverty, and he said: 'Seek forgiveness of Allah.' A third came complaining of childlessness, and he said: 'Seek forgiveness of Allah.' A fourth came complaining of crop failure, and he said: 'Seek forgiveness of Allah.'

When asked why he gave the exact same answer to completely different crises, Al-Hasan recited Ayahs 10–12 of Surah Nuh, explaining that Istighfar dissolves the spiritual barriers that withhold divine terrestrial provision.

Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab once went out to lead Salat al-Istisqa' (prayer for rain) and did nothing more than seek forgiveness from Allah repeatedly until rain descended. When questioned, he said: 'I have sought rain through the navigational stars of the sky by which rain is brought!'

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/301); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/233); Musannaf Ibn Abi Shaybah; Ihya' 'Ulum ad-Deen.

## Exegetical Deep Dive: Submerged in Water, Entered into Fire: Mimmā Khati'ātihim Ughriqoo fa-Udkhiroo Nāra
**Thematic Summary:** The instantaneous transition from earthly drowning to the retributive fire of the Barzakh.

In Ayah 25, Allah describes the fate of Noah's disobedient nation with breathtaking linguistic precision: 'Because of their sins they were drowned [Ughriqoo] and immediately entered into the Fire [fa-Udkhiroo Nāra], and they found no helpers besides Allah.'

Grammarians and exegetes, including Fakhr al-Din al-Razi and Al-Zamakhshari, emphasize the conjunction Fa (فَ) in 'fa-udkhiroo'. In Arabic balaghah, the Fa indicates 'At-Ta'qeeb bil-ittisal'—immediate consequence without temporal delay.

This verse serves as a decisive classical Sunni proof-text for the reality of 'Adhab al-Qabr' (the torment of the intermediate realm of Barzakh). The moment the suffocating floodwaters closed over their lungs, their disembodied souls were plunged directly into the blazing furnace of the grave.

They passed directly from catastrophic cold water into catastrophic heat, demonstrating that physical elements are instruments of sovereign justice and that no geographical distance separates worldly death from divine accounting.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir al-Tabari (23/640); Mafatih al-Ghayb (Al-Razi 30/144); Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/307); Sharh Usul I'tiqad Ahl as-Sunnah.

## Exegetical Deep Dive: Noah's Comprehensive Supplication: Rabbighfir Lee wa li-Walidayya
**Thematic Summary:** The exquisite concentric rings of intercession in the closing verse of Surah Nuh.

Surah Nuh concludes with one of the most beautiful and complete supplications in the entire Qur'anic corpus (71:28): 'My Lord, forgive me and my parents and whoever enters my house as a believer and the believing men and believing women. And do not increase the wrongdoers except in destruction.'

Imam al-Qurtubi analyzes the structural concentricity of this prayer: it begins with the individual soul (Rabbighfir lee), expands to the immediate biological lineage (wa li-walidayya), broadens to the domestic sanctuary and direct spiritual companions (wa li-man dakhala baytiya mu'mina), and culminates in the universal communion of all believing men and women across human history (walil-mu'mineena wal-mu'minat).

Classical scholars note that this verse teaches the etiquette of supplication: the seeker begins by purifying himself before the Lord, honors the profound debt owed to parents, protects the sanctity of the home, and unites his heart with the global Ummah.

The final clause ('wa la tazidi adh-dhalimeena illa tabara') seals the absolute rejection of tyranny, praying that entrenched oppressors who wage war on faith be stripped of all power to corrupt creation.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/238); Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (18/312); Tafsir al-Baghawi (8/230).
