# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 67 (Al-Mulk)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Interceding Shield of the Grave: The Thirty-Verse Advocate
**Thematic Summary:** How Surah Al-Mulk operates as Al-Mani'ah and Al-Munjiyah to shield the reciter from torment of the grave.

In canonical Sunni hadith literature recorded in Jami' at-Tirmidhi (2891) and Sunan Abi Dawud (1400) on the authority of Abu Hurairah, the Prophet ﷺ declared: 'Indeed, there is a surah in the Qur'an of thirty verses that interceded for a man until he was forgiven: Tabarakalladhi bi-yadihi'l-mulk.'

Abdullah ibn Mas'ud stated: 'Whoever recites Tabarakalladhi bi-yadihi'l-mulk every night, Allah will protect him by means of it from the torment of the grave. In the lifetime of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, we used to call it Al-Mani'ah (The Preventer).' (Sunan an-Nasa'i, Sahih at-Targhib 1475).

Classical commentators like Imam al-Qurtubi emphasize that this protection is not an automated amulet but is mediated through understanding its meanings, conforming to its ethical mandates, and maintaining regular nightly recitation anchored in deep reverence.

The Surah confronts the inquisitors in the Barzakh, interposing itself as an advocate for the servant who spent his worldly nights reflecting upon its cosmic verses.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2891 (Hasan Sahih); Sunan an-Nasa'i 10547; Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/205); Al-Mustadrak 'ala as-Sahihayn (2/498).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Flawless Seven Heavens: Ma Tara fee Khalqi ar-Rahmani min Tafawut
**Thematic Summary:** The architectural perfection of cosmic orbits and the exhaustion of the human analytical gaze.

In Ayah 3, Allah commands the human observer: 'Look again: do you see any rift (Futur)? Then return your gaze twice more; your gaze will return to you dazzled and exhausted.' The term 'Futur' denotes fissures, imbalances, or structural fatigue.

Imam Fakhr al-Din al-Razi in Mafatih al-Ghayb notes that the human mind naturally assumes that massive physical structures across vast spans of time inevitably suffer wear and tear. Yet celestial orbits operate with absolute mathematical precision.

Tafawut signifies incongruity, disproportion, or failure of alignment. By affirming that there is zero Tafawut in the creation of the Merciful, the Qur'an invites empirical investigation: the deeper astronomy investigates the universe, the more profound its underlying mathematical elegance appears.

The exhaustion of the eye ('Khasian wa huwa haseer') represents epistemological humility: finite human perception reaches its boundary while the boundless majesty of divine engineering remains pristine.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir al-Tabari (23/505); Mafatih al-Ghayb (Al-Razi 30/54); Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/176).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Crucible of Existence: Li-Yabluwakum Ayyukum Ahsanu 'Amala
**Thematic Summary:** Why death was created before life, and the classical definition of Ahsanu 'Amala.

In Ayah 2, Allah states: 'He who created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed.' Commentators analyze why death (Al-Mawt) is mentioned before life (Al-Hayah). Imam al-Qurtubi explains that non-existence preceded worldly life, or that death is the ultimate reminder that focuses human moral seriousness.

Ibn Kathir cites the profound definition of 'Ahsanu 'Amala' from the great sage Al-Fudayl ibn 'Iyad: 'It means the most sincere (Akhlasuhu) and the most correct (Aswabuhu).' When asked what this meant, he replied: 'If a deed is sincere but not correct, it is not accepted; and if it is correct but not sincere, it is not accepted. Sincere means that it is solely for Allah, and correct means that it is in accordance with the Sunnah.'

This dismantles the secular assumption that life is an arena for the maximization of quantity, wealth, or hedonistic consumption. The divine metric evaluates qualitative spiritual excellence and moral integrity.

The pairing of Al-'Azeez (The Almighty) with Al-Ghafoor (The Perpetual Forgiver) guarantees that while human lapses are inevitable in the crucible, divine mercy welcomes every sincere return.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/175); Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (18/207); Hilyat al-Awliya (8/95).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Sunk Water Inquest: Who Restores Your Gushing Aquifers?
**Thematic Summary:** The haunting closing question deconstructing technological hubris through hydrological vulnerability.

The Surah concludes with the piercing inquest: 'Say: Have you considered if your water were to sink deep into the earth [Ghawran], who then could bring you flowing water [Ma'in Ma'een]?'

Imam al-Tabari explains that 'Ghawran' signifies water that has sunk so deeply into subterranean fissures that neither shovels, buckets, nor drills can reach it. 'Ma'in Ma'een' is visible, gushing, sweet water running effortlessly upon the surface.

This verse addresses human vulnerability at its most fundamental biological axis. Human civilization can construct towering cities and advanced machines, but a three-day withdrawal of fresh water collapses all societal vitality.

Sunnah decorum records that when the reciter reaches this verse, it is recommended to proclaim with total humility: 'Allahu Rabbul-'Alameen' (Allah, the Lord of the Worlds, brings it to us!), acknowledging complete dependence upon divine sustenance.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir al-Tabari (23/519); Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/222); Sunan Abi Dawud; Tafsir al-Baghawi (8/182).
