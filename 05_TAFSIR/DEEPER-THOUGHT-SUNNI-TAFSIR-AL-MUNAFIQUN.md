# Classical Sunni Tafsir Monograph: Surah 63 (Al-Munafiqun)

**Exegetical Foundations:** Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari), Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Azim (Ibn Kathir), Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an (Qurtubi), Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi).

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Anatomy of Duplicity & Propped Timber (63:1–4)
**Thematic Summary:** Ka-annahum khushubun musannadah: handsome exteriors masking spiritual deadwood.

Ayahs 1 through 4 present an unmatched psychological autopsy of the hypocritical personality. They approach the Messenger with excessive declarations of faith: 'We bear witness you are Allah's Messenger!' Yet Allah unveils their inward fraud: 'Allah knows you are His Messenger, and Allah testifies the hypocrites are liars.' They deploy solemn religious oaths as a camouflage shield to obstruct justice.

Ayah 4 paints their physical presence: tall, statuesque, impeccably dressed, and possessing enchanting eloquence. 'And when they speak, you listen to their speech.' Yet behind this polished exterior, the Quran delivers its devastating assessment: 'They are as if they were propped up timbers (Khushubun Musannadah)'—lifeless beams of wood leaning against a wall, completely devoid of conscience, faith, or spiritual vitality.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari • Sahih Muslim • Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/128) • Mafatih al-Ghayb (Razi 30/15)

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Sedition at Muraysi' & Zayd's Vindication (63:5–8)
**Thematic Summary:** The boy whose ear was vindicated from above seven heavens.

In Sahih al-Bukhari (4900) and Sahih Muslim (2584), during the Expedition of Banu al-Mustaliq (Al-Muraysi'), a dispute arose at a well between an emigrant and an ally of the Khazraj. Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul exploded in rage, saying to his clan: 'Have they truly done this? They rival us in our own land! By Allah, the parable of us and these vagabonds of Quraysh is as the ancient saying: "Fatten your dog and it will devour you!" When we return to Medina, the more honorable will surely expel the humiliated!'

A young boy, Zayd ibn Arqam, heard this treason and reported it to the Prophet. Ibn Ubayy rushed to swear false oaths denying he ever said it, causing some elders to reproach Zayd. Zayd fell into deep grief until Surah Al-Munafiqun descended. The Prophet took Zayd by the ear and said: 'This is the boy whose ear Allah has vindicated!' When Ibn Ubayy's own son, Abdullah ibn Abdullah (a devout believer), heard what his father said, he drew his sword at the gates of Medina, barring his father from entering until he acknowledged that he was the humiliated and the Prophet was the honorable.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari 4900 • Sahih Muslim 2584 • Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3312 • Sirat Ibn Hisham

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Sovereign Monopoly of Honour: 'Izzah (63:8)
**Thematic Summary:** Wa lillahi al-'izzatu wa li-rasoolihi wa lil-mu'mineen: redefining dignity beyond tribalism.

Ayah 8 delivers the immortal response to all oligarchic arrogance: 'They say: "If we return to Medina, the more honorable will surely expel the humiliated!" But to Allah belongs all honour, and to His Messenger, and to the believers, but the hypocrites do not know.'

Umar ibn al-Khattab would later summarize this eternal principle: 'We were the most humiliated of people, and Allah honoured us with Islam. If we seek honour in anything other than that with which Allah honoured us, Allah will humiliate us!' Honour ('Izzah) is not a product of wealth, clan size, or political office; it is an unshakeable spiritual station granted exclusively to those whose hearts are bonded with the Sovereign Lord.

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Sahih al-Bukhari • Al-Mustadrak (Hakim) • Tafsir at-Tabari (23/390) • Hilyat al-Awliya

## Exegetical Deep Dive: The Agonizing Plea for Respite at Death (63:9–11)
**Thematic Summary:** Rabbi lawla akhkhartanee: why the dying yearn only to give charity.

The Surah closes with an urgent warning against spiritual drift: 'O you who have believed, let not your wealth or your children divert you from the remembrance of Allah. And whoever does that—it is they who are the losers.' The climax depicts the universal scene of death, where the dying soul cries in anguish: 'My Lord, if only You would delay me for a brief term so I would give charity and be among the righteous!'

Scholars like Ibn Abbas noted: 'Why does the dying person ask to give charity (Sadaqah) specifically, rather than pray, fast, or perform pilgrimage? Because of the monumental reward and forgiveness he sees attached to charity the instant the veil of mortality is lifted.' Yet the divine law is immutable: 'And never will Allah delay a soul when its appointed term has come.'

**Classical Exegetical Sources:** Tafsir Ibn Kathir (8/135) • Tafsir al-Qurtubi (18/115) • Jami' al-Bayan (Tabari)
