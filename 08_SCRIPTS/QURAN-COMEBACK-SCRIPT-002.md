# Episode 2 Flagship Script (`QURAN-COMEBACK-SCRIPT-002`)

**Campaign:** Come Back to the Qur'an  
**Title:** *"What Is the Qur'an Actually For?"*  
**Duration:** ~5 minutes (approx. 650 spoken words)  
**Tone:** Reflective, clarifying, grounding, compassionate  

---

## PRODUCTION SCRIPT & DIALOGUE

### 00:00 – 00:30 | SECTION 1: THE HOOK
**[VISUAL]**  
*A solitary traveler standing on an elevated ridge at dawn, looking across an immense desert horizon as gentle wind stirs the sand. Wide cinematic framing, spacious negative sky.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"If you ask ten people on the street what the Qur'an is, you’ll get ten different answers.

Some treat it like an ancient historical chronicle.  
Some treat it like a book of complex legal statutes.  
And some treat it like a decorative talisman—wrapped in velvet, placed on the highest shelf in the house, kissed when touched, but never actually read.

Have you ever stopped to ask: What did Allah Himself say this Book was for?"

---

### 00:30 – 01:30 | SECTION 2: THE AYAH & TRANSLATION
**[VISUAL]**  
*Macro camera gliding over an open page of Surah Al-Baqarah. Morning window light illuminates the first verses. Text appears with clean, spacious typography.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"Right at the very opening of the Qur'an, in the second verse of Surah Al-Baqarah, Allah defines His Book in five unforgettable words:

$$\text{ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ ۛ فِيهِ ۛ هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ}$$

*‘This is the Book! There is no doubt about it—a guide for those mindful of Allah.’*

Notice what Allah called it. He didn’t call it an encyclopedia. He didn’t call it an ornament for your shelf.  
He called it **Hudan**: *Guidance.*

A guide is not something you display on a wall. A guide is a map you consult when you are lost in the dark and need to know which step to take next."

---

### 01:30 – 03:00 | SECTION 3: TAFSIR & THE THORNY PATH
**[VISUAL]**  
*A stone pathway winding through a rugged landscape, morning mist hanging low. The camera moves forward at an unhurried, grounded pace.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"Ibn Kathir asks a penetrating question in his tafsir: The Qur'an was sent for all humanity, so why does Allah specify here that it is guidance *for those mindful of Allah—the Muttaqin*?

Because only someone who admits they need direction will actually follow the map. If you open the Qur'an looking for intellectual debates, you will find arguments. But if you open it with humility, asking for direction, you will find light.

What does it mean to be mindful? What is *Taqwa*?

Centuries ago, Umar ibn al-Khattab asked his companion Ubayy ibn Ka'b to describe it.  
Ubayy asked him: *‘Have you ever walked along a path covered in sharp thorns?’*  
Umar said: *‘Yes.’*  
Ubayy asked: *‘What did you do?’*  
Umar replied: *‘I gathered my clothes close to my body, and I walked with extreme care.’*  
Ubayy said: *‘That is Taqwa.’*

Taqwa isn’t hiding in a cave. Taqwa is living in this messy, chaotic world—with all its temptations, distractions, and moral traps—while walking carefully so your soul doesn't get torn."

---

### 03:00 – 04:15 | SECTION 4: TADABBUR — YOUR LIVING COMPASS
**[VISUAL]**  
*A person sitting by a wooden table in quiet morning stillness, reading a verse slowly, writing a single reflection in a journal.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"When you feel overwhelmed by your career, confused about your family, or uncertain about who you are becoming... you don't need another generic self-help book.

You need to consult your compass.

The Qur'an was sent to guide your ordinary days.  
It teaches you how to speak when you are angry.  
It teaches you how to treat your parents when you are tired.  
It teaches you what to do when grief settles into your ribs.  
It teaches you how to stand back up when you have fallen into sin.

It is guidance for the living."

---

### 04:15 – 05:00 | SECTION 5: PRACTICAL ACTION & QUIET CTA
**[VISUAL]**  
*Close-up of the open Mushaf. A hand gently turns to Surah Al-Baqarah. Fades to the calm Huurs brand end card.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"Today, change how you approach the Book of Allah.

Don't open it as an obligation you have to check off.  
Open it as someone who is lost, asking the Creator for direction.

Tonight, read just the first five verses of Surah Al-Baqarah.  
Pause at verse 2.  
And whisper from your heart: *‘Ya Allah, guide me today. I am listening.’*

Read it. Understand it. Live it.  
And come back tomorrow."
