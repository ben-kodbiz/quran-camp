# Episode 7 Flagship Script (`QURAN-COMEBACK-SCRIPT-007`)

**Campaign:** Come Back to the Qur'an  
**Title:** *"Why Does Allah Ask Us to Read Slowly?"*  
**Duration:** ~5 minutes (approx. 660 spoken words)  
**Tone:** Quiet, contemplative, grounding, deeply liberating  

---

## PRODUCTION SCRIPT & DIALOGUE

### 00:00 – 00:35 | SECTION 1: THE HOOK
**[VISUAL]**  
*Close-up of an hourglass on a wooden desk. Fine grains of sand falling slowly, one by one. In the soft background, an open Quran sits illuminated by a single warm oil lamp in a quiet room at night.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"We live in a world obsessed with speed.  
We listen to podcasts at 1.5x speed.  
We skim articles in twenty seconds.  
We rush through emails, traffic, conversations, and even our meals.

And without realizing it, we brought that frantic rush to the Holy Qur'an.

We open the Mushaf and our eyes dart to the bottom of the page:  
*‘How many pages left until the end of the juz? How fast can I finish this chapter?’*

We turn the Book of Allah into a checklist.  
And then we wonder why we closed the Book feeling as empty as when we opened it."

---

### 00:35 – 01:45 | SECTION 2: THE COMMAND IN THE NIGHT
**[VISUAL]**  
*The camera slowly drifts across the open calligraphy of Surah Al-Muzzammil. Soft night air gently moving a linen curtain near an open window overlooking a starry sky.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"In the earliest days of revelation, when the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was overwhelmed by the crushing burden of the message, Allah called him in the dead of night.

Surah Al-Muzzammil, verse 4:

$$\text{أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ وَرَتِّلِ الْقُرْآنَ تَرْتِيلًا}$$

*‘...and recite the Quran with measured, deliberate rhythm (tartīl).’*

Allah didn't say: *‘Finish as many chapters as possible before dawn.’*  
He said: **Rattil**—read it with *Tartīl*.

In classical Arabic, *Tartīl* doesn’t just mean beautiful singing.  
It comes from the image of evenly spaced teeth, or pearls spaced carefully on a thread.  
It means unhurried speech. Pronouncing every letter. Pausing after every verse. Giving the silence between words as much respect as the words themselves."

---

### 01:45 – 02:55 | SECTION 3: THE WARNING OF IBN MAS'UD
**[VISUAL]**  
*Macro shot of a hand turning a single textured cream page of the Qur'an with profound care. The rustle of paper in the quiet room.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"The great companion Abdullah ibn Mas'ud—one of the four men from whom the Prophet ﷺ commanded the companions to learn the Qur'an—saw people beginning to rush their recitation.  
He gave a warning that every single one of us needs to hear today:

*‘Do not scatter the Qur'an like poor dates tumbling from a tree.  
And do not chant it hurriedly like poetry.  
Stop at its wonders. Move your hearts with it.  
And let not the concern of any one of you be merely to reach the end of the surah.’*

Read that last sentence again:  
*Let not your concern be to reach the end of the surah.*

The goal of opening the Qur'an was never to reach the bottom of page twenty.  
The goal was to let even half a page reach the bottom of your heart."

---

### 02:55 – 04:00 | SECTION 4: TADABBUR — WHY SLOWNESS HEALS
**[VISUAL]**  
*Tranquil tracking shot across calm, still lake water at twilight reflecting pine trees and evening stars. A single drop falls, creating slow, expanding ripples across the mirror surface.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"In Surah Al-Furqan, the polytheists asked:  
*‘Why wasn’t this Qur'an revealed all at once in a single book?’*

And Allah answered:  
*‘We revealed it in deliberate stages so that We may strengthen your heart with it.’*

Fast information informs the intellect.  
Slow recitation transforms the soul.

When you rush through twenty verses, your brain processes the grammar, but your heart remains untouched.  
When you take a single ayah—like *‘Indeed, with hardship comes ease’*—and you repeat it three times...  
You breathe after each phrase...  
You ask yourself: *What does this mean for my worry right now?*  
That single verse becomes an anchor that holds you steady for an entire year.

One drop of rain absorbed into the soil gives life to a garden.  
A flash flood simply washes the topsoil away."

---

### 04:00 – 05:00 | SECTION 5: PRACTICAL ACTION & TONIGHT'S EXPERIMENT
**[VISUAL]**  
*The warm oil lamp softly illuminating a verse. A closed phone placed in a drawer. The atmosphere is quiet, serene, and grounded.*

**(VOICEOVER):**  
"Tonight, I invite you to try a simple experiment.

Put your phone in another room.  
Open the Qur'an. Do not aim for a juz. Do not even aim for a full page.  
Pick just two verses.

Recite the first verse out loud, slowly.  
Then close your eyes for thirty seconds.  
Let the meaning settle into your chest.  
Recite the second verse.  
Notice what happens to your breathing. Notice how the internal noise in your mind begins to quiet down.

Quality before quantity.  
Depth before distance.

Read it. Understand it. Live it.  
And come back tomorrow."
